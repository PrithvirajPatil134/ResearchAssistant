# Publication Playbook v4: Teaching Case Structural Patterns

**Synthesized from 14 Ivey/Harvard/SMU published cases (C2–C15)**
**Prof. Kakoli Sen's Case Writing Course, IIM Sambalpur**

**What's new in v4**: Cross-validated all three parallel batch analyses (Batch 1: C10–C15, C2; Batch 2: C3–C8; Batch 3: C9). Reconciled frequency counts, added closing-question taxonomy, refined weighted checklist, and tightened the Sai Tex application scaffold.

---

## 1. OPENING HOOK CONVENTIONS

Every case opens within the first two paragraphs. The protagonist is always named with full designation in the first narrative sentence.

| Hook Type | Count | Signature | Cases |
|-----------|-------|-----------|-------|
| Sensory-moment + tension | 9/14 | A sound, phone call, resignation letter, sleepless night — then protagonist + problem in one breath | C3 (WhatsApp beep), C6 (phone hung up, "white as a sheet"), C11 (award ceremony tears), C12 (resignation letter, "Not again!"), C15 (sleepless in bed) |
| Time-anchored declaration | 8/14 | "In [Month] [Year], [Name], [Title] of [Company]…" | C4 ("In January 2022, Aditya Agrawal…"), C7 ("In early 2022…NKC"), C9 ("Late one evening in January 2020, Nitin Lalit…"), C10 ("In February 2024, Susanne Pulverer…"), C14 ("In August 2018, Hitesh T.K.…") |
| Epigraph before narrative | 3/14 | Short quote (founder or literary) set apart, then protagonist enters | C5 (founder quote), C11 (Emerson), C12 (Roy T. Bennett) |
| Industry-context-first | 2/14 | Macro trend or sector data in paragraph 1, protagonist in paragraph 2 | C2 (AI/ML adoption in finance), C8 (supply chain forecasting) |

Overlap is common — 6/14 combine time-anchor + sensory-moment (e.g., C10 opens with a date AND a store closure decision; C9 combines "late one evening" + "sat in his office assessing").

**The dominant formula (12/14 cases):**

> [Date/Moment] + [Full Name, Title, Company] + [Emotional verb] + [Dilemma in one sentence]

Emotional verbs observed: pondered, wondered, stared, could not decide, exclaimed, lay sleepless, sat assessing, was tasked with.

### Protagonist Introduction Rules (14/14)

- Full name + title + company in the first narrative sentence
- Personal backstory woven into company history — never a standalone bio section
- If family/second-generation leader, that relationship established within page 1 (C3: Litisha as family legacy; C4: Aditya in family oil business; C7: NKC's village visits; C11: Jamana's son Viraj as digital force)

## 2. SECTION STRUCTURE & NARRATIVE ARC

### Section-Heading Taxonomy

Cases use 5–9 ALL-CAPS headings (Ivey) or title-case (SMU/Harvard). Headings cluster into six functional categories:

| Function | Common Headings | Frequency |
|----------|----------------|-----------|
| Company origin | COMPANY BACKGROUND, HOW IT BEGAN, GENESIS OF…, ABOUT [COMPANY] | 14/14 |
| Industry context | INDUSTRY AND MARKET CONTEXT, THE [INDUSTRY] MARKET IN INDIA, CARPET INDUSTRY IN INDIA | 13/14 |
| Operations/strategy | MARKETING MIX, ENTRY STRATEGY, SUSTAINABILITY AT…, SOURCING AT…, INNOVATION STRATEGIES | 11/14 |
| Problem escalation | THE CHALLENGES, THE QUALITY QUANDARY, THE PRICING PREDICAMENT, THE PROBLEM AT HAND | 12/14 |
| Decision/options | THE WAY FORWARD, THE DECISION IMPERATIVE, THINKING THINGS THROUGH, DECISION POINT | 13/14 |
| Competitive landscape | MAJOR PLAYERS, COMPETITORS, THE COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE | 8/14 |

### Canonical Architecture

| Section | Purpose | Length | Present |
|---------|---------|--------|---------|
| Opening Vignette (untitled) | Hook + protagonist + foreshadow dilemma | 1–2 pp | 14/14 |
| Company Background | Founding story, growth milestones, key people | 1–2 pp | 14/14 |
| Industry/Market Context | Market size, trends, competitive landscape | 1–2 pp | 13/14 |
| Core Problem/Challenge | Crisis with supporting data, escalation | 1–2 pp | 14/14 |
| Stakeholder Perspectives / Options | Differing views, 2–4 explicit alternatives | 1 pp | 12/14 |
| Decision Point / Way Forward | Protagonist must choose; no answer given | 0.5–1 pp | 13/14 |
| Exhibits | Data appendix after narrative | 2–5 pp | 14/14 |

### Narrative Arc

```
HOOK (tension) → CONTEXT (company + industry) → ESCALATION (problem deepens)
    → FORK (options laid out) → OPEN END (protagonist must decide)
```

C2 (Google SOX) is the sole case with a quasi-closed ending — governance/compliance topic where the framework itself is the "answer." C15 uses an (A)/(B) structure signaling a sequel. All other 12 cases leave the decision fully unresolved.

### Dimensions

| Metric | Range | Median |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Total pages | 6–17 | 10 |
| Word count | ~3,000–7,600 | ~4,500 |
| Narrative pages | 4–10 | 6 |
| Exhibit pages | 2–7 | 3 |

Outliers: C7 (Jaipur Rugs, 17pp) — social entrepreneurship spanning multiple artisan communities. C8 (Clean Air, 6pp) — focused quantitative/forecasting case where exhibits ARE the case. C14 (Vodafone Idea, 13pp) — IS integration complexity demands detailed technical context.

## 3. CLOSING-QUESTION FORMULAS

| Pattern | Count | Structure | Examples |
|---------|-------|-----------|----------|
| Explicit question battery | 8/14 | 2–4 direct questions, often "How could…?" or "Should…?" | C10: "Should IKEA India continue investing in small-format stores…?"; C11: "How could it strengthen the local market…?" |
| Reflective pause | 4/14 | Protagonist "wondered," "pondered," or "had to make a decision soon" | C9: "Nitin had to make a decision soon"; C15: "wondering what his next steps should be" |
| Options enumeration | 5/14 | Named alternatives or described paths in final paragraphs | C12: moral promise vs. business imperative; C15: internal transfer vs. wait vs. leave |

3 cases combine explicit questions with options enumeration (C10, C11, C15).

**Universal rule**: No case provides an answer. The last paragraph always leaves the protagonist at a decision point with genuine ambiguity.

## 4. EXHIBIT PATTERNS

### Volume

| Category | Cases | Count |
|----------|-------|-------|
| Light | C10, C11, C15 | 1–2 |
| Moderate | C2, C4, C6, C8, C12 | 2–5 |
| Heavy | C3, C7, C9, C13, C14 | 7–13 |
| **Median** | — | **5** |

### Type Frequency

| Type | Freq | Description |
|------|------|-------------|
| Financial data tables | 10/14 | Revenue, profit, cost breakdowns, P&L |
| Market/industry data | 7/14 | Market share, export data, sector size |
| Comparison tables | 5/14 | Competitor analysis, feature/price, integration options |
| Process/framework diagrams | 5/14 | Value chains, lifecycle stages, org charts, blueprints |
| Organizational charts | 4/14 | Reporting structures, team composition |
| Timeline/milestone tables | 3/14 | Career progression, company milestones |
| Product images/photos | 3/14 | Product photos (C11), store ambience (C12) |
| Email/document reproductions | 1/14 | C13: actual emails — unique to OB cases |

### Placement & Integration Rules (14/14)

- Exhibits always placed AFTER the full narrative — never inline
- In-text references: `(see Exhibit N)` — average 4 per case
- Numbered sequentially, titled in ALL CAPS
- Source attribution below every exhibit: "Source: Company documents" or "Source: Created by the case authors based on…"
- Financial exhibits almost always appear as Exhibit 1
- First exhibit referenced within the first 3 narrative pages

## 5. VOICE RULES

| Dimension | Rule | Compliance |
|-----------|------|------------|
| POV | Third-person omniscient | 13/14 (C15 uses closer third-person but still third) |
| Tense | Past tense ~80% (protagonist actions); present tense ~20% (industry facts only) | 14/14 |
| Tone | Journalistic, neutral — never advocates for any position | 14/14 |
| Quotes | Direct quotes sparingly (1–3 per case), always attributed | 14/14 |
| Numbers | Factual without adjectives ("increased 61%" not "an impressive 61%") | 14/14 |
| Theory | Embedded in situation, never named explicitly (save C2: NIST, C6: GHG Protocol) | 12/14 implicit, 2/14 named in passing, 0/14 textbook-style |
| Disclaimer | Ivey standard: "The authors do not intend to illustrate either effective or ineffective handling…" | 14/14 |

**The theory rule**: The case provides the raw situation. The teaching note maps it to theory. The case text itself never says "this is a Porter's Five Forces problem."

## 6. TENSION LEVERS

### Tension Types Across the Corpus

| Type | Cases | Core Conflict |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Survival threat | C6, C8 | Existential risk — lose a major client or go bankrupt |
| Growth vs. identity | C4, C5, C7, C9, C11 | Scale up without losing core values, quality, or craft |
| Innovation vs. control | C2, C3, C14 | Adopt new technology while managing risk and legacy systems |
| Ethics vs. business | C12, C15 | Social mission or personal integrity vs. operational/career reality |
| Strategic pivot | C10 | Abandon current strategy or double down on a failing format |
| Interpersonal conflict | C13, C15 | Workplace power dynamics, recognition, accountability |

### Six Design Patterns for Sustaining Tension (14/14)

1. **No right answer** — legitimate arguments for 2–4 paths; data supports multiple interpretations
2. **Real data for analysis** — financial exhibits enable quantitative discussion alongside qualitative judgment
3. **Stakeholder multiplicity** — minimum 3 stakeholders with conflicting interests (family, employees, investors, regulators, customers)
4. **Time pressure** — protagonist must decide "soon": a deadline, board meeting, regulatory notice, competitor move
5. **Personal stakes** — the decision affects the protagonist's career, family legacy, reputation, or livelihood
6. **Information asymmetry** — the protagonist (and therefore the student) doesn't have complete information

## 7. VARIATIONS & OUTLIERS

| Case | Deviation | Why It Works |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| C2 (Google SOX) | Quasi-closed ending; framework-heavy | Governance/compliance requires showing the control framework itself |
| C5 (Café Greenr) | Prof. Kakoli Sen as co-author | Demonstrates the course instructor's own publication standard — use as benchmark |
| C7 (Jaipur Rugs) | 17 pages, 10+ exhibits | Social entrepreneurship spanning artisan communities requires more context |
| C8 (Clean Air) | 6 pages, data-heavy, minimal narrative | Quantitative forecasting case — exhibits ARE the case |
| C13 (Communication) | Email reproductions as exhibits; OB focus | Actual communication artifacts are the evidence |
| C14 (Vodafone Idea) | 13 pages, 9 exhibits, technical IS integration | Telecom merger demands detailed technical context and comparison matrices |
| C15 (Dream Job) | Intimate POV, A/B structure, SMU publisher | Closer third-person builds empathy; "(A)" signals a sequel |

### Publisher Differences

| Publisher | Cases | Features |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| Ivey Publishing | C2–C14 (13) | ALL-CAPS headings, standard disclaimer, `(see Exhibit N)`, endnotes, currency footnote on first use |
| SMU/Harvard | C15 (1) | Title-case headings, more intimate voice, (A)/(B) structure, shorter format |

## 8. PUBLICATION READINESS CHECKLIST (Weighted)

### Structure (14/14 compliance — mandatory)

- [ ] Opens with specific date/moment: protagonist in a concrete setting facing the dilemma
- [ ] Full name + title + company in the first narrative sentence
- [ ] Dilemma foreshadowed in paragraph 1, fully developed in closing section
- [ ] 5–9 ALL-CAPS section headings (title-case for SMU/Harvard)
- [ ] 4,000–5,500 words narrative + 3–7 exhibits (target 8–12 total pages)
- [ ] Ends with 2–4 open questions OR reflective pause with unresolved tension

### Narrative Quality (13–14/14 compliance)

- [ ] Third-person past tense throughout; present tense only for industry context
- [ ] No theoretical framework names in case text (save for teaching note)
- [ ] Neutral tone — no advocacy, no editorializing on data
- [ ] Protagonist's emotional state signaled but not melodramatic
- [ ] Direct quotes sparingly (1–3), always attributed
- [ ] Numbers factual without adjectives

### Exhibits (14/14 compliance)

- [ ] All exhibits placed after narrative, never inline
- [ ] Numbered sequentially, titled in ALL CAPS
- [ ] Source attribution below every exhibit
- [ ] Financial summary as Exhibit 1 (if applicable)
- [ ] In-text `(see Exhibit N)` references (minimum 3)
- [ ] At least one financial table, one market/industry exhibit, one comparison or process exhibit

### Pedagogical Design (12–14/14 compliance)

- [ ] 2–4 explicit strategic alternatives (no "right answer")
- [ ] Minimum 3 stakeholders with conflicting interests
- [ ] Time pressure / urgency established
- [ ] Personal stakes for protagonist (career, family, livelihood)
- [ ] Sufficient data for both quantitative and qualitative discussion
- [ ] Decision point genuinely ambiguous — reasonable students will disagree

### Publication Mechanics (14/14 compliance)

- [ ] Ivey-standard copyright disclaimer and authorship statement
- [ ] "The authors do not intend to illustrate either effective or ineffective handling" disclaimer
- [ ] Currency amounts defined with exchange rate footnote on first use
- [ ] Endnotes for all factual claims, data sources, and quotes
- [ ] No fabricated data — all exhibits sourced from company documents or public sources

## 10. APPLICATION: SAI TEX LTD. (ICHALKARANJI CASE)

### Recommended Opening Hook

Sensory-moment + time-anchor (the dominant 64% pattern), mirroring C3's WhatsApp hook and C15's sleepless-night tension:

> *October 2025. The night shift at Sai Tex Ltd. was silent. Avinash Kisanrao Patil stood alone on the factory floor, the ten Rifa Rapier machines idle around him. His phone showed two notifications: a ₹1,20,000 interest debit from the bank, and a WhatsApp message from his eldest worker, Shivmani Sharma, asking if there would be work next week.*

### Section Map (target 8–10 pp + 4 exhibits)

| # | Heading | Content | Length |
|---|---------|---------|--------|
| 1 | *(Untitled vignette)* | AKP on factory floor; ₹3L revenue vs. ₹4L costs; the dilemma | 1.5 pp |
| 2 | THE MANCHESTER OF MAHARASHTRA | Ichalkaranji cluster: 50,000+ looms, migrant labor shift, export dependency | 1.5 pp |
| 3 | SAI TEX: A DECADE OF WEAVING | Founded 2010, 10 Rifa machines at ₹12L each, job-work at 18 paisa/pick | 1.5 pp |
| 4 | THE PERFECT STORM | US tariff → 40% export drop → local competition → job rates below breakeven | 1.5 pp |
| 5 | THREE PATHS FORWARD | Option A: Green Technical Pivot; Option B: Hybrid Lean; Option C: Exit | 1.5 pp |
| 6 | THE DECISION | Bank requires bungalow as collateral; family opposes more debt; 18 workers' livelihoods | 0.5 pp |

### Exhibit Plan

| Exhibit | Type | Source |
|---------|------|--------|
| 1 | Monthly P&L breakdown | AKP actuals: ₹3L revenue, ₹1.2L interest, ₹80K electricity, ₹2L payroll |
| 2 | Green Pivot projection | Air-jet at ₹35/mtr vs. current ₹10/mtr; 4x speed; green subvention at 10% vs. 14% |
| 3 | Ichalkaranji cluster data | 50,000 ordinary looms, 12,000 air-jet/rapier, 3,000 auto looms |
| 4 | Strategic options comparison | Investment, risk, debt impact, timeline, worker impact for all 3 options |

### Tension Levers

| Lever | Sai Tex | KB Parallel |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| Survival threat | ₹1L monthly loss, 14% interest on ₹1.2Cr debt | C6, C8 |
| Growth vs. identity | Job-work tradition vs. Sale-Purchase pivot | C4, C7 |
| Family/SEW stakes | Bungalow as collateral, family opposes more debt | C4, C9 |
| Worker welfare | 18 families dependent, Shivmani's 10-year loyalty | C7, C12 |
| Information gap | No balance sheet; AKP tracks via personal ledger | Authentic classroom ambiguity |
