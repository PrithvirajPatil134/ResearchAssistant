## Thesis Statement

The agentic AI governance research requires building theory where none exists. CRO-taught methodology (Eisenhardt protocol, Sutton-Staw criteria) provides the procedural scaffolding. QNTR content (Kolt's framework, Vu's empirical base, Mikalef's measurement precedent) provides the domain substance. DBA institutional constraints (paradigm positioning, thesis structure) provide the framing discipline. This synthesis connects these three streams into a coherent Phase 1 qualitative design: theoretical sampling of firms with mature versus immature agentic AI governance, construct table development following Eisenhardt's protocol, and formative measurement specification following Diamantopoulos and Mikalef's precedents. The co-authored meta-analysis with Prof. Prashar runs as a parallel workstream that feeds domain specification for the governance maturity construct.

## Evidence From Sources

### CRO Methodology: How to Build Theory

Eisenhardt and Graebner (2007) prescribe theory-building from cases when existing research "does not address the research question at all, or does so in a way that is inadequate" (p. 26). The method works through replication logic: "each case serves as a distinct experiment that stands on its own as an analytic unit" (p. 25). Cases are selected for theoretical reasons, not representativeness.

[Source: CRO/wiki/sources/eisenhardt-graebner-2007-theory-building-opportunities.md, §Key Arguments]

Sutton and Staw (1995) set the quality standard for the resulting theory. It must explain *why* governance maturity reduces enterprise risk, not merely show *that* it does. "Theory is about the connections among phenomena, a story about why acts, events, structure, and thoughts occur" (p. 378). The five substitutes (references, data, variables, diagrams, hypotheses) are traps that Phase 1 must avoid.

[Source: CRO/wiki/sources/sutton-staw-1995-what-theory-is-not.md, §Key Arguments, What Strong Theory Is]

### QNTR Content: What to Build Theory About

Kolt (2025) provides the domain framework. The governance challenge for AI agents is structurally a principal-agent problem: information asymmetry (principals cannot determine agent competencies), discretionary authority (instructions are inevitably incomplete), and loyalty failures (agents may not pursue self-interest but still fail to serve users' interests). Conventional mechanisms (incentive design, monitoring, enforcement) fail because "AI agents operate at speeds and scales beyond human oversight capacity" (pp. 33-36).

[Source: QNTR/wiki/sources/kolt-2025-governing-ai-agents.md, §Key Arguments]

Kolt's three governance principles (inclusivity, visibility, liability) are the candidate construct dimensions, but they remain normative. No operational definitions or measurement items exist.

[Source: QNTR/wiki/sources/kolt-2025-governing-ai-agents.md, §Limitations]

Vu et al. (2025) provide the closest empirical base: six governance recommendations from 22 practitioner interviews. These include business context definition, legal/ethical guardrails, human-agent collaboration design, customised autonomy levels, risk management, and safe integration with fallback mechanisms. But none of the participants had actual agentic AI experience; their "assessments are based on expectations, often against the backdrop of existing RPA technologies" (§4).

[Source: QNTR/wiki/sources/vu-2025-agentic-bpm-governance.md, §Key Arguments, §Summary]

### DBA Framing: Paradigmatic Positioning

Rehman and Alharthi (2016) define the four components of a research paradigm: ontology, epistemology, methodology, methods. The agentic AI governance research occupies a pragmatist position (not covered in their introductory treatment but implied by their conclusion that paradigm choice should follow "the necessities and requirements of a research study rather than the obdurate insistence of adhering to one particular philosophical outlook," p. 51).

[Source: CRO/wiki/sources/rehman-alharthi-2016-research-paradigms.md, §Key Arguments, Conclusion]

Phase 1 aligns with interpretivist assumptions: knowledge is constructed from executive discourse; reality is socially constructed through governance practices. Phase 2 aligns with post-positivist assumptions: relationships between constructs can be tested statistically; reality exists independently but is apprehended imperfectly (critical realism).

[Source: CRO/wiki/sources/rehman-alharthi-2016-research-paradigms.md, §Key Arguments, Interpretivism and Positivism sections]

### Measurement: The Formative Construct Challenge

Prof. Prashar identifies that "Agentic AI governance maturity" as an IV "sounds like a formative construct, which might require a different set of analysis techniques than the usual SEM and regression."

[Source: QNTR/wiki/entities/prof-prashar.md, §Key Guidance]

The formative-construct concept page confirms that governance maturity meets all four decision criteria: indicators are conceptually distinct, non-overlapping, need not correlate, and have different antecedents. "Omitting an indicator potentially alters the nature of the construct" (Diamantopoulos 2011, p. 337). Content validity requires "a census of indicators, not a sample" (p. 337), which is precisely what Phase 1 must produce.

[Source: QNTR/wiki/concepts/formative-construct-measurement.md, §Application to Research, Why Governance Maturity Is Formative]

## How Sources Connect

The three streams converge on a single Phase 1 design:

**CRO provides the process**: Eisenhardt's seven steps (justify, sample, instrument, enter field, within-case, cross-case, propose). Sutton-Staw provides the quality gate (every proposition must explain the causal mechanism).

**QNTR provides the content**: Kolt's principal-agent framing defines *what* to look for in cases (information asymmetry, authority scope, loyalty conflicts). Vu's six recommendations offer candidate codes for the deductive coding layer. The formative measurement literature defines *how* the resulting constructs must be specified.

**DBA provides the structure**: pragmatist paradigm justifies the sequential mixed-method design. Phase 1 (interpretivist, inductive, theory-building) feeds Phase 2 (post-positivist, deductive, hypothesis-testing). Each phase is evaluated by its own quality criteria (credibility/transferability for Phase 1; validity/reliability for Phase 2).

The connection is not merely additive. Without CRO's protocol, the qualitative work risks becoming an unstructured fishing expedition. Without QNTR's domain knowledge, the cases would lack theoretical direction. Without DBA's paradigmatic discipline, the methodology chapter would not hold together under doctoral examination.

## Implications for Research

### Phase 1 Qualitative Design

**Theoretical sampling (polar types)**: Select 4-8 firms across two poles:
- *Mature governance*: firms with documented agent oversight policies, established audit trails for AI agent decisions, defined authority boundaries, incident response protocols, formal accountability structures.
- *Immature governance*: firms deploying agentic AI with ad hoc oversight, no formal protocols, autonomous decision-making without monitoring, reliance on individual developer judgment.

The contrast maximises construct clarity. Per Eisenhardt and Graebner: "adding three cases to a single-case study is modest in terms of numbers, but offers four times the analytic power" (p. 28).

[Source: CRO/wiki/sources/eisenhardt-graebner-2007-theory-building-opportunities.md, §Key Arguments, Theoretical Sampling]

**Diverse informants**: CIOs/CTOs, Chief Risk Officers, compliance officers, line managers who interact with AI agents. "It is unlikely that these varied informants will engage in convergent retrospective sensemaking" (Eisenhardt and Graebner, p. 28). Critical requirement: participants must have *actual* experience deploying or governing AI agents (not RPA-benchmarked expectations as in Vu et al.).

[Source: CRO/wiki/sources/eisenhardt-graebner-2007-theory-building-opportunities.md, §Key Arguments, Managing Interview Data Bias; QNTR/wiki/sources/vu-2025-agentic-bpm-governance.md, §Limitations]

**Construct table development**: The key Phase 1 deliverable. Columns: construct name, definition, polar case evidence (mature firm vs. immature firm), proposed Phase 2 operationalisation. Rows: each governance practice dimension identified through cross-case analysis. Per Eisenhardt and Graebner, these tables "summarize the case evidence and indicate how the focal construct is 'measured,' thus increasing the 'testability' of the theory" (p. 29).

[Source: CRO/wiki/sources/eisenhardt-graebner-2007-theory-building-opportunities.md, §Key Arguments, Presenting Empirical Evidence]

**Formative measurement approach**: The construct table must produce indicators that satisfy formative specification criteria. Phase 1 effectively serves as the content validation stage recommended by Diamantopoulos: identifying the full set of governance practices that constitute the construct before Phase 2 measurement. Non-significant indicators may be retained if theoretically justified (per Mikalef and Gupta's practice).

[Source: QNTR/wiki/concepts/formative-construct-measurement.md, §Application to Research, Practical Implications]

### The Sutton-Staw Check

Every proposition emerging from Phase 1 must answer: *why* does this governance practice reduce enterprise risk? The mechanism must be specified, not just the relationship.

Example of a mechanism: Visibility practices (agent activity logging, real-time surveillance, audit access) reduce risk *because* they collapse the information asymmetry between human principals and AI agents that Kolt identifies as the structural source of governance failure. Without visibility, principals cannot evaluate agent performance; with visibility, deviations from intended behavior are detectable before they cause incidents.

This is theory: a causal story. A mere prediction ("firms with visibility practices have fewer incidents") is a hypothesis, not theory (Sutton and Staw, p. 377).

[Source: CRO/wiki/sources/sutton-staw-1995-what-theory-is-not.md, §Key Arguments; QNTR/wiki/sources/kolt-2025-governing-ai-agents.md, §Key Arguments on information asymmetry]

### Parallel Workstream: Meta-Analysis with Prof. Prashar

Prof. Prashar has offered to co-author a meta-analysis on "Agentic AI Governance and Enterprise Risk." This feeds Phase 1 in a specific way: the meta-analytic search produces the systematic evidence base for domain specification of the governance maturity construct. It identifies all prior studies that have measured both governance-related variables and risk/performance outcomes (possibly "in multiple confusing ways," per Prashar's feedback) and examines boundary conditions.

[Source: QNTR/wiki/entities/prof-prashar.md, §Key Guidance, future direction; §Communication History, 2026-05-07]

The meta-analysis is not a prerequisite for Phase 1 cases. The two can proceed in parallel: case interviews build construct dimensions from lived experience, while the meta-analysis validates those dimensions against the cumulative quantitative literature. Convergence strengthens both; divergence identifies what practice has learned that the literature has not yet captured.

Open questions awaiting Prashar's reply: scope of meta-analytic search (agentic AI only vs. broader IT/AI governance), target venue, collaboration format.

## Open Questions

1. What is the minimum case count for the polar-types design? Eisenhardt (1989b) suggests 4-10 cases for theory building. For two poles (mature/immature), 3-4 per pole is likely sufficient.
2. How do Kolt's three principles (inclusivity, visibility, liability) map onto Papagiannidis et al.'s three practice categories (structural, relational, procedural)? Visibility is procedural; inclusivity is relational; liability is structural. But this mapping needs Phase 1 validation.
3. Should the pragmatist paradigm be stated explicitly in the methodology chapter, or is it sufficient to describe the sequential design and let the paradigm remain implicit? DBA examination conventions may require explicit positioning.
4. Is CBSEM or PLS-SEM the right approach for Phase 2? Diamantopoulos recommends CBSEM for formative constructs (error term estimation, global fit, model comparison). Mikalef and Gupta published successfully with PLS-SEM in an A* journal. The choice depends on achievable sample size.
5. Can the meta-analysis with Prashar run fast enough to inform Phase 1 coding? If the systematic search identifies validated governance sub-dimensions that align with Phase 1 interview findings, the construct table gains cumulative-evidence strength.
