# Questionnaire Survey Design

## When to Use

Use questionnaire surveys when the research requires:
- Quantitative data from a defined population [src/research_assistant/spaces/DBA/knowledge/Marketing & Commerce in Intnl Context/L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.3]
- Broad coverage (local, national, international) [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.6]
- Anonymity for respondents (especially for sensitive or ego-related questions) [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.6]
- Economical data collection from a large population [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.6]
- Clear-cut, precise results factually reflecting the situation under study [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.3]

Do NOT use when:
- The research needs to uncover causes or relationships for attitudes, beliefs, and actions (questionnaires cannot do this) [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.7]
- Clarification of ambiguous questions is required during data collection [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.7]
- Verification of response accuracy is essential [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.7]

## Design Steps (9-Step Process)

1. Define the problem/survey topic
2. Use previous studies & expert advice
3. Define research questions/objectives
4. Define study population and sample size
5. Develop a preliminary questionnaire
6. Pretest the questionnaire (similar subjects)
7. Cover letter (introducing the study, deadline for return, guarantees for data anonymity)
8. Stamped & self-addressed envelope (or digital equivalent)
9. Follow-up letter for non-respondents

## Delivery Modes

Five delivery modes: mail, web-based, group, e-mail, interview. [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.5]

## Question Types

### By Purpose

| Type | What It Measures | Example Pattern |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Factual | Age, gender, education, experience | Demographics; often used to investigate relationships |
| Informative | What respondents know about a topic | "How do you come to know about...?" |
| Attitudinal/Opinion | Beliefs, feelings, values | "Do you agree that...?" |
| Self-perception | Self-comparison with others | "How active are you...?" / "How will you describe your...?" |
| Standard of Action | How respondents will act in a situation | "For which party will you vote...?" |
| Projective | Indirect expression via imposing feelings on others | "Are most Singaporeans quitters or stayers?" |

[src/research_assistant/spaces/DBA/knowledge/Marketing & Commerce in Intnl Context/L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, pp.8-9]

### By Structure

- **Unstructured (open-ended)**: Free responses; useful for exploratory studies; difficult to analyze; low response rate [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.10]
- **Structured (closed-ended)** [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.11]:
  - Dichotomous: yes/no, true/false, agree/disagree
  - Multiple choice: check all that apply, or check only one
  - Contingency: qualifies respondent for subsequent questions (filter/skip logic)

## Scaling Responses

### Likert-Type Scale

Assigns numeric ranking on a continuum with predetermined units. Designed to show differentiation among respondents' opinions. Common patterns: importance (very important to unimportant), adequacy (very adequate to inadequate), frequency (frequently to infrequently). [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.12]

### Semantic Differential Scale

Bipolar adjective pairs on a 7-point scale (e.g., Bad 1-2-3-4-5-6-7 Good). [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.13]

### Rank-Order / Comparative Ranking Scale

Respondents rank items by importance or preference. Should be used carefully, as many respondents have difficulty understanding/responding. Only use a short list of responses. [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.14]

## Question Placement Principles

- **Opening**: Start with easy, non-threatening questions [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.15]
- **Sequence**: According to research topic; maintain logical flow [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.15]
- **Sensitive questions**: Ask only after trust is developed [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.15]
- **Difficult questions**: Place toward the end [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.15]
- **Personal data** (age, gender, qualification, experience): Placement at end [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.15]

## Mapping Questions to Research Objectives

Every survey question should trace back to a research objective. A visual mapping structure ensures each research objective is supported by multiple questions. [L7 Questionnaire survey LECTURE.pdf, p.20]
