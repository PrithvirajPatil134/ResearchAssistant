# Publication Playbook: Ivey/Harvard Teaching Case Patterns

**Based on analysis of 14 published cases (C2–C15) from Ivey Publishing and SMU/Harvard**

---

## 1. NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

### Opening Hook (First 2 Paragraphs)
| Pattern | Frequency | Examples |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Scene-setting with protagonist in moment of tension** | 9/14 (64%) | C3: "Beep-beep! ...casually tapped open a new WhatsApp message"; C6: "hung up the phone. She was white as a sheet"; C15: "Tyler Lim lay sleepless in bed" |
| **Time-anchored opening** ("In [Month] [Year]...") | 8/14 (57%) | C4: "In January 2022, Aditya Agrawal...was considering a critical issue"; C10: "In February 2024, Susanne Pulverer..." |
| **Epigraph/Quote before narrative** | 3/14 (21%) | C5: founder quote; C11: Emerson quote; C12: Roy T. Bennett quote |
| **Contextual/Industry-first opening** | 2/14 (14%) | C2: starts with AI/ML industry context before protagonist |

**Dominant pattern**: Time-stamp + protagonist name + title + company + immediate tension. The protagonist is ALWAYS named with full designation in the first paragraph.

### Protagonist Introduction
- Full name + title + company in opening sentence (14/14)
- Personal backstory woven into company history (not a separate bio section)
- Emotional state signaled early: "pondered," "wondered," "stared," "could not decide"

### Context Building (Middle 60–70% of case)
Typical section flow:
1. **Company Background** — founding story, growth trajectory, key milestones
2. **Industry/Market Context** — market size, trends, competitive landscape
3. **Specific Challenge Setup** — the operational/strategic problem with data
4. **Stakeholder Perspectives** — differing views from partners, employees, board

### Decision Point
- Appears in opening paragraph (foreshadowed) AND closing section
- Always framed as 2–3 explicit strategic alternatives
- Protagonist must choose; no "right answer" is given
- Common closing phrases: "wondered how," "had to decide," "the decision was complex and multifaceted"

### Ending Style
| Type | Frequency | Description |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Open-ended with explicit questions** | 10/14 (71%) | Ends with 2–4 rhetorical questions the protagonist faces |
| **Reflective pause** | 3/14 (21%) | Protagonist pondering, no explicit questions but clear unresolved tension |
| **Summary conclusion** | 1/14 (7%) | C2 (governance case) — wraps up with lessons, atypical |

## 2. PAGE LENGTH & SECTION BREAKDOWN

| Metric | Range | Median |
|--------|-------|--------|
| **Total pages** | 6–17 | 10 |
| **Word count** | ~3,000–7,600 | ~4,500 |
| **Narrative pages** | 4–10 | 6 |
| **Exhibit pages** | 2–7 | 3 |

### Typical Section Count: 6–9 sections

**Standard section pattern:**
1. Opening vignette (untitled or case title) — 1–2 pages
2. Company Background — 1–2 pages
3. Industry/Market Context — 1–2 pages
4. Core Problem/Challenge — 1–2 pages
5. Stakeholder/Strategic Options — 1 page
6. Way Forward / Decision Point — 0.5–1 page
7. Exhibits — 2–5 pages

## 3. EXHIBITS

### Volume
| Cases | Exhibit Count |
|-------|--------------|
| Light (C10, C11, C15) | 1–2 exhibits |
| Moderate (C2, C4, C6, C8, C12) | 2–5 exhibits |
| Heavy (C7, C9, C13, C14) | 7–13 exhibits |
| **Median** | **5 exhibits** |

### Types of Exhibits Used
| Type | Frequency | Examples |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Financial data tables** | 10/14 | Revenue, profit, cost breakdowns |
| **Process/framework diagrams** | 5/14 | Value chains, lifecycle stages, org charts |
| **Market/industry data** | 7/14 | Market share, export data, segmentation |
| **Product images/photos** | 3/14 | Product photos, store ambience |
| **Comparison tables** | 5/14 | Competitor analysis, feature comparisons |
| **Organizational charts** | 4/14 | Reporting structures, team composition |
| **Timeline/milestone tables** | 3/14 | Career progression, company milestones |
| **Email/document reproductions** | 1/14 | C13: actual emails as exhibits |

### Placement & Integration Rules
- Exhibits are ALWAYS placed AFTER the full narrative (never inline)
- In-text references use "(see Exhibit N)" — average 4 references per case
- Exhibits are numbered sequentially and titled in ALL CAPS
- Source attribution appears below every exhibit
- Financial exhibits almost always appear as Exhibit 1

## 4. VOICE & TENSE

### Point of View
- **Third-person omniscient**: 13/14 cases (93%)
- **Limited first-person**: Only through direct quotes or epigraphs
- C15 uses more intimate third-person (close to protagonist's thoughts)

### Tense
- **Past tense dominant**: All 14 cases use past tense for narrative ("was," "had," "decided")
- **Present tense**: Used ONLY for industry facts/context ("The market is..." / "India has...")
- **Ratio**: ~80% past tense, ~20% present tense for contextual statements

### Tone
- Journalistic and neutral — never advocates for a position
- Descriptive but not literary — no metaphors or flowery language
- Standard disclaimer: "The authors do not intend to illustrate either effective or ineffective handling"

## 5. THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK INTEGRATION

### Key Finding: Frameworks are IMPLICIT, not explicit

| Approach | Frequency | How It Works |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Embedded in situation** | 12/14 (86%) | Case describes a value chain problem without naming "Porter's Value Chain" |
| **Named in passing** | 2/14 (14%) | C2 mentions NIST AI Risk Framework; C6 references GHG Protocol |
| **Textbook-style explanation** | 0/14 (0%) | No case teaches theory directly |

### Common Implicit Frameworks
- **Blue Ocean Strategy**: C5 (Café Greenr — untapped plant-based market)
- **Stakeholder Theory**: C6 (Walmart supplier sustainability pressure)
- **Social Entrepreneurship**: C7 (Jaipur Rugs — community impact vs. profit)
- **Ansoff Matrix**: C9 (Alpha Advantech — expansion vs. consolidation)
- **Omnichannel Strategy**: C10 (IKEA India — store format decisions)
- **Brand Building/Equity**: C4, C5, C11 (quality vs. price positioning)
- **Change Management**: C14 (Vodafone-Idea IS integration)
- **Organizational Behavior**: C13, C15 (workplace dynamics, leadership)
- **DEI/Inclusion**: C12 (transgender employee retention)
- **Supply Chain/Operations**: C8 (forecasting models)

**Rule**: The case provides the RAW SITUATION. The instructor's teaching note maps it to theory. The case itself never says "this is a Porter's Five Forces problem."

## 6. TENSION & PEDAGOGICAL VALUE

### What Creates Tension

| Tension Type | Cases | Description |
|-------------|-------|-------------|
| **Survival threat** | C6, C8 | Existential risk — lose major client or go bankrupt |
| **Growth vs. identity** | C4, C5, C7, C9, C11 | Scale up without losing core values/quality |
| **Innovation vs. control** | C2, C3, C14 | Adopt new technology while managing risk |
| **Ethics vs. business** | C12, C15 | Social mission vs. operational reality |
| **Strategic pivot** | C10 | Abandon current strategy or double down |
| **Interpersonal conflict** | C13, C15 | Workplace power dynamics, recognition, fairness |

### Pedagogical Design Patterns
1. **No right answer**: Every case presents legitimate arguments for multiple paths
2. **Real data for analysis**: Financial exhibits enable quantitative discussion
3. **Stakeholder multiplicity**: 3+ stakeholders with conflicting interests
4. **Time pressure**: Protagonist must decide "soon" — urgency is always present
5. **Scalability of discussion**: Cases work for both surface-level and deep analytical discussion

## 7. PUBLICATION-READY CHECKLIST FOR SAI TEX LTD.

Based on the patterns above, a case about an Indian textile MSME facing modernization should:

### Structure
- [ ] Open with a specific date/moment: protagonist in their office/factory facing the dilemma
- [ ] Name + title + company in first sentence
- [ ] Foreshadow the decision in paragraph 1, fully develop it in the closing section
- [ ] 6–8 ALL-CAPS section headings
- [ ] Target 4,000–5,500 words narrative + 3–5 exhibits
- [ ] End with 2–3 open questions the protagonist must answer

### Sections to Include
1. Opening vignette (protagonist + tension)
2. Company Background (founding, growth, family dynamics)
3. Indian Textile/MSME Industry Context (market data, government schemes, competition)
4. The Modernization Challenge (specific technology/process decisions)
5. Financial & Operational Constraints (working capital, skill gaps, infrastructure)
6. Stakeholder Perspectives (family members, workers, buyers, government)
7. The Decision / Way Forward (explicit alternatives, open-ended)

### Exhibits
- [ ] Financial summary table (revenue, margins, costs — 3–5 years)
- [ ] Industry/market data (Indian textile MSME sector size, growth)
- [ ] Process comparison (traditional vs. modern manufacturing)
- [ ] Competitor landscape table
- [ ] Organizational/family structure chart

### Voice & Style
- [ ] Third-person past tense throughout narrative
- [ ] Present tense only for industry context paragraphs
- [ ] No theoretical framework names in the text
- [ ] Neutral tone — no advocacy for any option
- [ ] Include Ivey-standard copyright disclaimer and authorship statement
- [ ] "(see Exhibit N)" references woven into narrative naturally

### Tension Levers for Sai Tex
- Family legacy vs. modernization investment
- Quality/craft tradition vs. cost-competitive mass production
- Limited MSME capital vs. technology adoption costs
- Skilled artisan workforce vs. automation
- Local/domestic market vs. export opportunity
- Government scheme eligibility vs. compliance burden
