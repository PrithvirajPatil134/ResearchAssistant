## Definition

A research paradigm is a set of philosophical assumptions that guides how a researcher understands reality, produces knowledge, designs inquiry, and selects techniques. Rehman and Alharthi (2016) decompose it into four components: ontology (beliefs about what exists), epistemology (how knowledge is acquired and validated), methodology (the logic connecting research questions to methods), and methods (specific techniques for data collection and analysis).

[Source: wiki/sources/rehman-alharthi-2016-research-paradigms.md, §Key Arguments]

## Key Dimensions

### Four Components

| Component | Question it answers | Example |
|-----------|-------------------|---------|
| Ontology | What is the nature of reality? | Single objective reality vs. multiple constructed realities |
| Epistemology | How can we know that reality? | Objective observation vs. subjective interpretation |
| Methodology | What logic connects questions to methods? | Deductive (theory-first) vs. inductive (data-first) |
| Methods | What tools collect and analyze data? | Surveys, interviews, experiments, documents |

Ontology and epistemology are "to research what 'footings' are to a house: they form the foundations of the whole edifice" (p.51, citing Grix 2004).

[Source: wiki/sources/rehman-alharthi-2016-research-paradigms.md, §Key Arguments]

### Three Paradigms

**Positivism / Post-Positivism**
- Ontology: Realism. Reality exists independently, governed by laws. Post-positivism (critical realism) acknowledges reality "can only be apprehended imperfectly because of the complexity of social phenomena" (p.54).
- Epistemology: Objectivism. Researcher observes without contaminating.
- Methodology: Deductive. Hypothesize then test.
- Methods: Experiments, surveys, statistical analysis.
- Quality: Internal validity, external validity, reliability, objectivity.

**Interpretivism**
- Ontology: Anti-foundationalist. "Truth and reality are created, not discovered" (p.55).
- Epistemology: Subjective. Knowledge is co-constructed between researcher and participant.
- Methodology: Inductive. Discover patterns, collapse under themes.
- Methods: Open-ended interviews, observations, document analysis.
- Quality: Credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability.

**Critical Theory**
- Ontology: Historical realism. Reality shaped by cultural, political, and power structures.
- Epistemology: Subjective and transformative.
- Methodology: Dialogic and dialectical. Aims to change, not merely explain.
- Methods: Critical ethnography, action research, discourse analysis.
- Quality: Degree to which oppressive structures are exposed and redressed.

[Source: wiki/sources/rehman-alharthi-2016-research-paradigms.md, §Key Arguments]

### Pragmatism (Not in Source, Noted as Gap)

Rehman and Alharthi do not cover pragmatism, which is the philosophical foundation for mixed-methods research. Pragmatism rejects the paradigm wars by holding that the research question determines method choice. This gap is acknowledged as a limitation of the source paper.

## How Sources Relate

This is a single-source concept page promoted to shared because paradigmatic grounding applies across all research spaces. The paper's concluding argument is directly relevant: researchers should not treat paradigms as "three religions" but should choose approaches "guided by the necessities and requirements of a research study" (p.58). This anti-dogmatic stance supports the pragmatist position that underpins mixed-methods designs.

The concept connects to Sutton and Staw's "What Theory Is Not": paradigms determine how knowledge is produced, while Sutton-Staw determines what counts as a theoretical contribution within any paradigm. Both interpretivist and positivist studies must explain *why*, not merely describe *what*.

## Application to Research

For the agentic AI governance paper (exploratory sequential mixed-methods design):

**Phase 1 (qualitative, theory-building)**:
- Aligns with interpretivist assumptions. Knowledge about governance practices is constructed from executive discourse, regulatory filings, and practitioner interviews.
- Methodology is inductive: governance constructs emerge from data.
- Quality criteria: credibility (member checking), transferability (thick description), dependability (audit trail), confirmability (reflexivity).

**Phase 2 (quantitative, hypothesis-testing)**:
- Aligns with post-positivist assumptions. Constructs developed in Phase 1 are tested on firm-level survey data.
- Methodology is deductive: propositions derived from theory are confirmed or rejected.
- Quality criteria: internal validity (endogeneity controls), external validity (sample representativeness), reliability (measurement model), objectivity (replicability).

**Integration**: The sequential design is justified by pragmatism: governance constructs do not yet have validated measures (per Prof. Prashar's feedback), so qualitative development must precede quantitative testing. The paradigm shift between phases is deliberate, not contradictory.
