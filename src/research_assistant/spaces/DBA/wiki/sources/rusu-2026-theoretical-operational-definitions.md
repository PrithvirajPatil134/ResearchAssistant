# L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions - Lecture (Rusu 2026)

## Key Arguments

The lecture establishes that the two basic components of a theory are **constructs** and **linkages**, and that clearly defining constructs is "the first step in developing good theory." [source_path, pp.2-3]

Definitions must communicate how a construct has meaning both **conceptually** (via theoretical definitions that provide generalizability) and **empirically** (via operational definitions that provide testability). [source_path, pp.5-6]

Four criteria for good definitions are presented: clarity, non-tautology, explicit dimensionality, and non-overlap with other constructs in the theory. [source_path, p.4]

The lecture introduces the Bacharach (AMR, 2001) framework mapping the theory-level vocabulary (constructs, propositions) to the empirical-level vocabulary (variables, hypotheses). Theoretical definitions describe constructs; measures describe variables. Propositions link constructs; hypotheses link variables. [source_path, pp.16-17]

Not applicable (pedagogical lecture deck, not empirical research). Uses brand loyalty as a worked example across three progressively richer theoretical definitions paired with operational definitions. [source_path, pp.7-15]

## Key Findings

The main pedagogical point is that richer, more dimensionally-specified theoretical definitions yield richer (multi-dimensional) operational definitions. The brand loyalty example moves from a single-dimension "preference" definition (yielding simple behavioral measures) to Jacoby and Chestnut's (1978) six-dimension definition yielding multi-dimensional operational measures including purchase sequences, decision-unit types, alternative availability, and conscious choice. [source_path, pp.7-15]

A summary table (p.15) shows seven different theoretical definitions of brand loyalty from the literature, each paired with its corresponding operational definition, illustrating the range of conceptual-to-empirical mappings possible for a single construct.

## Relevance to Research

This lecture provides the methodological scaffolding for the construct-definition phase of any empirical or mixed-methods thesis. For the DBA thesis on organizational restructuring and GenAI, it applies directly to:

- Defining "organizational structure" with dimensionality (what range, what axes)
- Avoiding tautological definitions of GenAI adoption
- Ensuring proposed constructs do not conceptually overlap
- Deriving operational definitions suitable for archival coding from the theoretical definitions
