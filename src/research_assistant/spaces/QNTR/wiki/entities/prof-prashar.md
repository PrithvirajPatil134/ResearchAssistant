## Key Guidance

- Writing is "good, crisp and clear" but "too direct." Avoid direct negatives; focus on what past studies could do better. Do not criticise individual studies; discuss the focal relationship from all angles. In the final paragraph, describe what the literature misses.
  [Source: `spaces/QNTR/feedback/response_prof_prashar_termpaper_review_20260505.md`, §Argumentation and Positioning]

- Use a gap table: a table of past studies with the last row showing what your study does and how it bridges the gap. Reference: Han et al. (2017) in Journal of Marketing for exemplar.
  [Source: `spaces/QNTR/feedback/response_prof_prashar_termpaper_review_20260505.md`, §Argumentation and Positioning]

- Use more market evidence (McKinsey, Deloitte reports). Current draft uses only one instance ("firms with mature IT governance earn returns on IT investment up to 40 percent higher than peers").
  [Source: `spaces/QNTR/feedback/response_prof_prashar_termpaper_review_20260505.md`, §Argumentation and Positioning]

- Literature review should constitute 20-30% of the thesis write-up.
  [Source: `spaces/QNTR/feedback/response_prof_prashar_termpaper_review_20260505.md`, §Literature Review]

- Phase 1 of method should be a literature review step (SLR, narrative review, or meta-analysis of the focal relationship).
  [Source: `spaces/QNTR/feedback/response_prof_prashar_termpaper_review_20260505.md`, §Method]

- If construct measures do not exist, developing them becomes the key aim of the qualitative study. If they do exist, cite the papers that developed them and explain how they will be used.

- "Agentic AI governance maturity" as IV sounds like a formative construct, requiring different analysis techniques than standard SEM and regression.
  [Source: `spaces/QNTR/feedback/response_prof_prashar_termpaper_review_20260505.md`, §Method]

- Contribution section must start with "Discussion" (compare with past work, reason out contradictory findings), then switch to Theoretical Contribution and Managerial Implications.
