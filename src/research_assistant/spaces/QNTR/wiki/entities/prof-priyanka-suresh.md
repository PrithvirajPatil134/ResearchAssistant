## Key Guidance

### Literature Review Types (March 21 session)

- **Thematic Review**: preferred for very new topics
- **Systematic Literature Review (SLR)**: most popular; avoid if topic is new; preferred when topic is mature with plentiful accessible materials
- **Semi-Systematic Literature Review**: for topics that are not mature
- **Meta-analytic review**: for mature topics with plentiful materials
- **Bibliometric review**: must be combined with another review type; insufficient alone for publication

[Source: `Prof. Priyanka Class Notes.docx`, paragraphs under "03/21"]

### Quality Gates

- Check ABDC score/rank of papers and/or impact factor
- Avoid blogs, low-tier journals, or predatory publishers
- Use peer-reviewed, high-quality sources only
- Review should reflect rigor, credibility, and relevance
- Aim for ABDC or higher
- Conference papers acceptable only if the field is nascent and lacks supporting research papers in top journals (example given: Agentic Governance)

[Source: `Prof. Priyanka Class Notes.docx`, under "Identifying and Evaluating Relevant Literature"]

### Search Strategy (March 28 session)

- **Building Block Strategy**: useful when topic is interdisciplinary
  - Step 1: Identify key concepts (break research question into components)
  - Step 2: Find synonyms and related terms
  - Step 3: Broad search (each concept separately)
  - Step 4: Moderate specificity (combine two blocks)
  - Step 5: Narrow the search once foundational knowledge established
- Boolean operators must be uppercase (AND, OR, NOT)
- Use quotation marks for exact phrase searching (e.g., "agentic governance")
- Truncation with * for variations (e.g., educat*)
- Document all searches executed to avoid repetition

[Source: `Prof. Priyanka Class Notes.docx`, paragraphs under "03/28"]

### PRISMA Framework

- Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses
- Refer to PRISMA templates; includes checklist for abstract content

[Source: `Prof. Priyanka Class Notes.docx`, under "PRISMA Framework"]

### Synthesis Approach (April 4 session)

- Synthesize, do not merely summarize
- Synthesizing = summarizing + critiquing + adding originality and birds-eye-view opinion
- Synthesis is topic-based, not author-based
- Usually 40 references in literature survey; 12-20 sufficient if synthesized properly
- Refer to existing literature review papers to identify gaps
- Use concept maps for visual representation of research gaps
- Journals favour concept maps in literature reviews

[Source: `Prof. Priyanka Class Notes.docx`, paragraphs under "04/04" and general synthesis notes]

### Structuring Options

- **Thematic structuring**: organizing by recurring themes, studies grouped by topic
- **Theoretical structuring**: organizing around theoretical frameworks; use when multiple frameworks exist, research crosses theoretical boundaries, or theory development is central
