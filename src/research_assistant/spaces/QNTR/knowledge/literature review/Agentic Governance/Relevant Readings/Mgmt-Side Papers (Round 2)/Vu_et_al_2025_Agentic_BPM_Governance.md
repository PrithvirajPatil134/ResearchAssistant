# Vu et al. (2025) — Agentic Business Process Management: Practitioner Perspectives on Agent Governance in Business Processes

**Source:** arXiv:2504.03693v2 (also published as conference paper in Lecture Notes in Business Information Processing, LNBIP vol. 565, pp. 29-43, August 2025)

**Authors:** Hoang Vu (SAP), Nataliia Klievtsova (TU Munich), Henrik Leopold (Kuhne Logistics University), Stefanie Rinderle-Ma (TU Munich), Timotheus Kampik (SAP/Umea University)

**Stats:** 1,245 accesses, 8 citations

## Key Findings

### Method
- Qualitative study: 22 semi-structured interviews with BPM practitioners from diverse industries
- Qualitative content analysis (Mayring and Fenzl, 2019) with deductive-inductive coding
- None of the 22 participants had actual practical experience with agentic AI; assessments are based on expectations

### Definition of Agentic BPM
"Agentic Business Process Management (ABPM) describes (i) the deployment and execution of autonomous software agents to achieve business process goals and (ii) the application of agent-based abstractions for the process-oriented design and analysis of autonomous software agents."

### Six Governance Recommendations
1. **Business context** — Define specific goals, clarify expected benefits, assess short/long-term costs
2. **Guardrails** — Ensure agents operate within legal, ethical, and organizational boundaries
3. **Human-agent collaboration** — Establish clear roles and responsibilities, identify when human intervention is necessary
4. **Customization** — Tailor autonomy levels, oversight mechanisms, documentation requirements, transparency
5. **Risk management** — Implement safety measures to monitor performance and prevent undesired agent evolution
6. **Adoption** — Ensure seamless integration with existing processes, implement fallback mechanisms

### Four BPM Alignment Actions
1. **Human-agent balance** — Design workflows leveraging both AI capabilities and human expertise
2. **Human involvement** — Clarify whether humans collaborate as peers or take supervisory roles
3. **Process structures** — Evolve beyond static process models to flexible, dynamic structures
4. **Performance metrics** — Develop governance frameworks tracking agent impact on efficiency, decision quality, outcomes

### Key Practitioner Insights
- Benefits: efficiency, data quality, compliance, scalability, democratization of process data
- Risks: bias, over-reliance, cybersecurity threats, job displacement, unauthorized decision-making
- Configurable autonomy recommended: flexibility in low-risk areas, restriction in high-risk scenarios
- Gradual approach: start simple, increase autonomy as trust grows
- Responsibility for failures is shared: organizations, developers, business leaders

### Limitations (stated by authors)
- Small sample, predominantly multinational organizations
- Concept of agentic AI still formative; varied terminology across participants
- Limited adoption constrains interpretation
- No quantitative validation

### Future Work (stated by authors)
- Empirical validation of conceptual frameworks
- Quantitative studies across industries
- Robust governance structures for transparent, accountable decision-making
- Examine how agentic AI governance should differ from RPA governance
