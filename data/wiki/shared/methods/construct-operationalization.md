# Construct Operationalization: Theoretical and Operational Definitions

## When to Use

When developing theory or designing empirical research that requires moving from abstract concepts to measurable variables. Specifically:

- Defining constructs in a conceptual framework so they are clear, non-tautological, and dimensionally specified [src/research_assistant/spaces/DBA/knowledge/Marketing & Commerce in Intnl Context/L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions  LECTURE.pdf, p.4]
- Bridging from theory-level reasoning (constructs, propositions) to empirical testing (variables, hypotheses) [L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions LECTURE.pdf, p.16]
- Justifying measurement choices in a methodology chapter by showing how operational definitions derive from theoretical ones [L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions LECTURE.pdf, pp.5-6]

## Core Concepts

### Two Components of Theory

A theory has two basic components: **constructs** and **linkages**. Constructs are "the entities that make up the subject matter of a theory." Definitions of constructs are "very important" and "the first step in developing good theory is clearly defining the constructs." [L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions LECTURE.pdf, pp.2-3]

### Criteria for Good Definitions

Good definitions must satisfy four criteria [L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions LECTURE.pdf, p.4]:

1. **Clear and concise**
2. **Not tautological**: do not use a term (or various forms of it) to define itself
3. **Clearly identify the dimensionality of the construct and the nature of that dimension** (e.g., market share ranges from 0% to 100%)
4. **Avoid conceptual overlap** with the meaning of other constructs in the theory

### Theoretical vs. Operational Definitions

Definitions communicate how a construct has meaning both **conceptually** and **empirically**. This is achieved through two types [L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions LECTURE.pdf, pp.5-6]:

| | Theoretical Definitions | Operational Definitions |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Provide theories with their **generalizability** | Provide theories with their **empirical meaning** |
| **Function** | Define constructs in terms that allow the theory to relate to other theories and other contexts | Define constructs in terms that allow theories to be empirically tested |
| **Level** | Abstract, conceptual | Concrete, measurable |

Note: a distinction exists between operational definitions and measures, though for simplicity they are treated as equivalent in introductory usage. [L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions LECTURE.pdf, p.6]

The lecture presents a terminological mapping between theory-level and empirical-level elements, attributed on the slide to "Bacharach, AMR 2001" [L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions LECTURE.pdf, pp.16-17]. (1989), "Organizational Theories: Some Criteria for Evaluation," Academy of Management Review, 14(4), 496-515 [verified via JSTOR vol.14 no.4 Oct 1989; AOM DOI 10.5465/amr.1989.4308374]:

| Theory Level | Empirical Level |
|---|---|
| Theoretical definitions describe **CONSTRUCTS** | Measures describe **VARIABLES** |
| Linkages between constructs are **PROPOSITIONS** | Linkages between variables are **HYPOTHESES** |

The relationship is visualized as: Construct, via theoretical definition, maps to Variable via operational definition/measure. Propositions link constructs; hypotheses link variables. [L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions LECTURE.pdf, p.17]

## Worked Example: Brand Loyalty

The lecture provides three progressively richer theoretical definitions of brand loyalty, each paired with operational definitions, to illustrate dimensionality and measurement mapping [L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions LECTURE.pdf, pp.7-15]:

**Definition #1** (simple): "Brand loyalty is a person's consistent preference for the product(s) under a specific brand name." Operational: (a) supporting consumption by others, (b) occasional or habitual purchasing, (c) refusal to consume other brands. [p.7]

**Definition #2** (attitudinal-behavioral): "Brand Loyalty is a biased behavioural response, expressed as (1) degree of positive attitude, (2) commitment, and (3) intention to continue purchasing." Operational: purchase behaviour (what they actually buy). [p.8]

**Definition #3** (Jacoby and Chestnut, 1978, p.80): "Brand loyalty is the (a) biased, (b) behavioral response, (c) expressed over time, (d) by some decision-making unit, (e) with respect to one or more alternative brands out of a set of such brands, and (f) is a function of psychological (decision-making, evaluative) processes." Operational: multi-dimensional measures including (1) non-random bias, (2) actual purchases, (3) sequences of purchases, (4) consumers/agent buyers, (5) buyer alternatives exist, (6) conscious decisions. [pp.9-14]

The progression from Definition #1 to #3 illustrates increasing dimensional specificity and how richer theoretical definitions generate richer (multi-dimensional) operational definitions. [L3 Theoretical & Operational Definitions LECTURE.pdf, p.15]

## Application to Thesis Research

For the DBA thesis on organizational restructuring and GenAI, this method applies when:

1. Defining theoretical constructs (e.g., organizational structure quality, GenAI adoption maturity) with clarity, non-tautology, explicit dimensionality, and non-overlapping conceptual boundaries
2. Moving from those theoretical definitions to operational definitions suitable for archival coding of annual reports or survey measurement
3. Articulating propositions at the construct level, then deriving testable hypotheses at the variable level following the Bacharach framework
