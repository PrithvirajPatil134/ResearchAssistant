# Quantitative Research & Questionnaire Survey (Rusu 2026)

## Overview

Course lecture on quantitative research and questionnaire survey design, delivered by Dr. Claudia-Roxana Rusu as part of the Marketing & Commerce in International Context module, UB DBA 2026 programme. Covers the full lifecycle of questionnaire-based research: definition, steps, delivery modes, advantages/disadvantages, question types, response scaling, question placement, data analysis tools, and mapping questions to research objectives.

## Key Content

### Definition of Quantitative Research

Quantitative research "typically involves counting or measuring," "often statistical tests are applied to detect or confirm trends," and its "purpose is to get clear-cut, precise and accurate results, factually reflecting the situation under study." The questionnaire is identified as "one of the popular tools for the quantitative research." [source_path, p.3]

### Questionnaire Survey Steps (9-Step Process)

1. Define the problem/survey topic
2. Use previous studies & expert advice
3. Define research questions/objectives
4. Define study population and sample size
5. Develop a preliminary questionnaire
6. Pretest the questionnaire (similar subjects)
7. Cover letter (introducing the study, deadline for return, guarantees for data anonymity)
8. Stamped & self-addressed envelope
9. Follow-up letter for non-respondents

[source_path, p.4]

### Delivery Modes

Five questionnaire delivery modes are identified: mail, web-based, group, e-mail, and interview. [source_path, p.5]

### Advantages of Questionnaires

- Broad coverage (local, national, international)
- Frank and anonymous answers
- Economical for large population
- Less time consuming
- Quantitative data (easy to analyze)
- Filling at a convenient time
- Easy to respond (fixed responses)
- Good for sensitive & ego-related questions
- Original hard-to-obtain data

[source_path, p.6]

### Disadvantages of Questionnaires

- No clarification for ambiguous questions
- Inadequate motivation to respond
- Poorly worded or direct questions
- Unattractive style and format
- Low response rate
- Inaccurate responses, no verification
- Limited fixed responses
- Cannot uncover causes or relationships for attitudes, beliefs, actions

[source_path, p.7]

### Types of Questions

Six question types are presented:

1. **Factual**: Age, gender, education, experience. Often used to investigate relationships [source_path, p.8]
2. **Informative**: What respondents know about a given topic [source_path, p.8]
3. **Attitudinal/Opinion**: Beliefs, feelings, values of respondents [source_path, p.8]
4. **Self-perception**: Allows subjects to compare their ideas or actions with others [source_path, p.8]
5. **Standard of Action**: How respondents will act in a situation [source_path, p.9]
6. **Projective Questions**: Allow respondents to answer indirectly by imposing feelings/attitudes/beliefs on others [source_path, p.9]

### Structured vs. Unstructured Questions

- **Unstructured (open-ended)**: Respondents reply freely; useful for exploratory studies; usually difficult to analyze; usually low response rate [source_path, p.10]
- **Structured (closed-ended)**: Provide a group of fixed responses [source_path, p.11]
  - Dichotomous: yes/no, true/false, agree/disagree
  - Multiple choice: check all that apply, or check only one
  - Contingency: determine if respondent is qualified to answer a subsequent question

[source_path, pp.10-11]

### Scaling Responses

Three scaling approaches are covered:

1. **Likert-type Scale**: Each response assigned a numeric ranking based on a continuum with predetermined units of measurement; designed to show differentiation among respondents' opinions. Examples: "How important is..." (very important to unimportant), "How adequate are..." (very adequate to inadequate), "How frequently do you..." (frequently to infrequently) [source_path, p.12]

2. **Semantic Differential Scale**: Provides a set of bipolar adjective pairs on a 7-point scale (e.g., Bad-Good, Unfair-Fair, Harsh-Gentle) [source_path, p.13]

3. **Rank-order/Comparative Ranking Scale**: Respondents rank responses according to importance/preference. Should be used carefully as many respondents face difficulty understanding/responding. Only use a short list of responses. [source_path, p.14]

### Question Placement Principles

- Opening questions: start with easy, non-threatening questions
- Sequence: according to research topic, logical flow
- Sensitive questions: asked only after trust is developed
- Difficult questions: more difficult questions at the end
- Personal data: age, gender, qualification, experience

[source_path, p.15]

### Data Analysis Tools

The lecture lists tools organized by category:

- **Statistical Software**: SPSS (survey data, regression, ANOVA, descriptive statistics), SAS (advanced analytics and predictive modeling), Stata (econometrics and panel data analysis) [source_path, p.16]
- **Programming Languages**: R (statistical modeling, visualization, machine learning), Python (data cleaning, analysis, predictive modeling; libraries: Pandas, NumPy, Scikit-learn) [source_path, p.16]
- **Spreadsheet Tools**: Microsoft Excel (pivot tables, descriptive stats, basic regression), Google Sheets (collaborative analysis) [source_path, p.17]
- **Data Visualization**: Tableau (interactive dashboards), Power BI (business intelligence and KPIs) [source_path, p.17]
- **Survey & Market Research Platforms**: Qualtrics (advanced survey design and analytics), SurveyMonkey (basic survey analysis) [source_path, p.18]

### Components of Empirical Research

Nine components listed: problem statement/research questions/purposes/benefits; theory/assumptions/background literature; variables and hypotheses; operational definitions and measurement; research design and methodology; instrumentation/sampling; data analysis; conclusions/interpretations/recommendations. [source_path, p.19]

### Mapping Survey Questions to Research Objectives

A visual diagram shows research objectives 1, 2, and 3 each mapped to multiple survey questions, illustrating that every survey question should trace back to a research objective. [source_path, p.20]
