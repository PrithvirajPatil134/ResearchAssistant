## Summary

A 54-page research agenda document that frames the field of AI governance as the problem of "devising global norms, policies, and institutions to best ensure the beneficial development and use of advanced AI." Organises the field into three research clusters.
[Source: GovAI-Research-Agenda.pdf, p. 2, Abstract]

## Structure (Three Research Clusters)

1. **Technical Landscape** (pp. 15-33): mapping technical possibilities, assessing AI progress, AI safety. Asks what technical inputs, possibilities, and constraints exist for AI.
2. **AI Politics** (pp. 34-47): domestic politics, international political economy, international security. Focuses on political dynamics between firms, governments, publics, researchers.
3. **AI Ideal Governance** (pp. 48-51): values and principles, institutions and mechanisms, positive visions. Envisions what structures we would ideally create.

[Source: GovAI-Research-Agenda.pdf, pp. 4-5, Table of Contents; p. 2, Abstract]

## Key Concepts Relevant to DBA Thesis

### Safety Production Function (p. 31)

Asks what inputs produce safety in AI systems. The author's governance maturity construct can be read as a firm-level operationalisation of this: what organisational inputs (visibility, liability, guardrails) produce reduced risk?
[Source: GovAI-Research-Agenda.pdf, p. 31; interpretation from `data/summaries/qntr_litreview_knowledge_synthesis_v2.md`, §5]

### Normal Accidents in AI Systems (p. 26)

Cites the 2010 flash crash as an example of cascading algorithmic failure. Supports the enterprise risk framing: autonomous systems interacting can produce emergent failures not predictable from individual component behaviour.
[Source: GovAI-Research-Agenda.pdf, p. 26; interpretation from `data/summaries/qntr_litreview_knowledge_synthesis_v2.md`, §5]

### Race Dynamics (pp. 43-45)

Describes how competitive pressure between nations (or firms) to deploy AI first creates corner-cutting on safety. Maps to the "shadow AI" construct: firms racing to deploy agents without governance replicate at the enterprise level what Dafoe describes at the international level.
[Source: GovAI-Research-Agenda.pdf, pp. 43-45; interpretation from `data/summaries/qntr_litreview_knowledge_synthesis_v2.md`, §5]

### Verification (pp. 46-47)

How can third parties verify that AI systems meet safety and governance standards? Maps to the visibility and auditability constructs in the thesis framework.
[Source: GovAI-Research-Agenda.pdf, pp. 46-47; interpretation from `data/summaries/qntr_litreview_knowledge_synthesis_v2.md`, §5]

## Scope and Limitations

- Written in 2018, before "agentic AI" as currently defined (autonomous tool-using, goal-directed systems). The governance challenges described are at the macro/international level, not the firm-level operational governance that the thesis targets.
- The document is a research agenda (poses questions, maps territory) rather than an empirical study. It does not produce testable propositions or data.
- Transfer to the agentic case requires careful scoping: Dafoe's "governance" is about global norms and institutions; the thesis's "governance" is about firm-level control mechanisms over AI agents.

[Source: GovAI-Research-Agenda.pdf, pp. 1-3, Preface; interpretation from `data/summaries/QNTR_additional_readings_synthesis.md`, §1.1]
