## Folder: `Mgmt-Side Papers (Round 2)/`

These papers address the management/IS framing of AI governance, firm performance, and organisational design for autonomous systems.

### 1. Berente et al. (2021) — Managing AI

- **Filename:** `Managing Artificial Intelligence_Berente-Gu-Recker-Santhanam_2021_A*_7.pdf`
- **Journal:** MIS Quarterly, 45(3), 1433-1450
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.25300/MISQ/2021/16274
- **ABDC:** A*
- **JCR IF:** ~7.0 (Q1)
- **Type:** Editorial / Conceptual (special issue introduction)
- **Relevance:** Frames the IS-discipline mandate for managing autonomous AI. Distinguishes AI-as-tool from AI-as-autonomous-actor. Calls for research on governance of AI that acts with bounded autonomy — the agentic case. Provides the disciplinary anchor for why governance-to-risk questions matter in IS research.

### 2. Lanzolla et al. (2025) — Rethinking AI Agents: A Principal-Agent Perspective

- **Filename:** `Rethinking AI Agents- A Principal-Agent Perspective_Lanzolla-Dahlander-others_2025_A_9-7.pdf`
- **Journal:** California Management Review, 67/4 or online first (July 2025)
- **URL:** https://cmr.berkeley.edu/2025/07/rethinking-ai-agents-a-principal-agent-perspective/
- **ABDC:** A
- **JCR IF:** ~9.7 (Q1)
- **Type:** Conceptual / Practitioner-oriented
- **Relevance:** Directly applies principal-agent theory to AI agents in organisations. Argues AI agents should be viewed as "guided actors" requiring governance structures that balance autonomy and accountability. Validates the theoretical lens used in the term paper (Kolt's principal-agent framing) from a management journal perspective. Closest ranked-journal equivalent to Vu et al.'s practitioner framing.

### 3. Holgersson et al. (2024) — Open Innovation in the Age of AI

- **Filename:** `Open Innovation in the Age of AI_Holgersson-Dahlander-Chesbrough-Bogers_2024_A_9-7.pdf`
- **Journal:** California Management Review, 67(1), 5-20
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/00081256241272191
- **ABDC:** A
- **JCR IF:** ~9.7 (Q1)
- **Type:** Conceptual
- **Relevance:** Examines how AI (including autonomous systems) changes governance of innovation processes. Connects to P2 (workflow control rigidity) and the broader question of how firms structure human-agent collaboration in business processes.

### 4. Recker et al. (2023) — Orchestrating Human-Machine Designer Ensembles

- **Filename:** `Orchestrating Human-Machine Designer Ensembles During Product Innovation_Recker-vonBriel-Yoo-Nagaraj-McManus_2023_A_9-7.pdf`
- **Journal:** California Management Review, 65(3), 27-47
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/00081256231160627
- **ABDC:** A
- **JCR IF:** ~9.7 (Q1)
- **Type:** Empirical (case study)
- **Relevance:** Studies how organisations orchestrate human-AI collaboration during product innovation. Directly addresses the human-agent collaboration governance recommendation from Vu et al. Examines how firms balance autonomy and control when AI participates in creative workflows — maps to P4 (inverted U-shape between control rigidity and risk).

## Folder: `Empirical Foundation Papers/`

These papers provide the empirical baseline for automation governance and risk.

### 5. Eulerich et al. (2024) — The Dark Side of RPA

- **Filename:** `The Dark Side of Robotic Process Automation- Understanding Risks and Challenges with RPA_Eulerich-Waddoups-Wagener-Wood_2024_A_3-1.pdf`
- **Journal:** Accounting Horizons, 38(2), 143-152
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2308/HORIZONS-2022-168
- **ABDC:** A
- **JCR IF:** ~3.1 (Q2 in Accounting)
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Relevance:** Empirically examines risks and governance challenges of RPA in organisations. Vu et al. position agentic AI as the successor to RPA. Documents specific failure modes (30-50% project failure rates, compliance risks, resistance to automation) that P1 and P3 aim to address at the agentic level. Provides the empirical baseline for what governance failure looks like in the automation predecessor to agentic AI.

### 6. Haase et al. (2024) — Interdisciplinary Directions for RPA and LLMs

- **Filename:** `Interdisciplinary Directions for Researching Effects of RPA and LLMs on Business Processes_Haase-Kremser-Leopold-Mendling-Onnasch_2024_A_2-2.pdf`
- **Journal:** Communications of the Association for Information Systems (CAIS), 54, 579-604
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.17705/1CAIS.05422
- **ABDC:** A
- **JCR IF:** ~2.2 (Q2)
- **Type:** Research agenda / Conceptual
- **Relevance:** Co-authored by Leopold (also on Vu et al.). Bridges RPA and LLM-based automation, examining how the shift from deterministic bots to stochastic AI agents changes governance requirements. Calls for interdisciplinary research combining BPM, organisational behaviour, and human factors — the methodological space the mixed-methods design occupies. Directly sets up the research gap that Vu et al. and the term paper address.
