## Summary

Argues that firms must learn to orchestrate ensembles of human and machine designers during product innovation. Differentiates machine designers along two dimensions: core innovation capability (search/discovery vs. generation) and process sovereignty (supervised vs. unsupervised), yielding four types (Figure 1, p. 30). Proposes four orchestration aspects: ensemble configuration, sequencing, problem domain, and innovation goal (Table 1, p. 36).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 4 §Core Argument]

## Key Arguments

### Four Types of Machine Designers (Figure 1, p. 30)

Based on core capability x sovereignty:
1. **Parameterized construction** (Hackrod) - supervised, generative
2. **Solution pattern discovery** (Moderna) - supervised, search
3. **Automated use case identification** (Starbucks) - unsupervised, search
4. **Product-based learning** (Ubisoft) - unsupervised, generative

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 4 §Key Findings, pp. 31-33]

### Four Orchestration Aspects (Table 1, p. 36)

1. Ensemble configuration
2. Sequencing (human-first vs. machine-first)
3. Problem domain
4. Innovation goal

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 4 §Key Findings]

### Ethical and Governance Implications

"Sovereignty decisions carry moral implications, human designers must stay in the loop for accountability and legitimacy" (pp. 41-42). "Firms must rethink how they govern innovation activities" (p. 40). Data privacy tensions identified: between data privacy and portability, consent models and data use, user participation and algorithmic bias (pp. 42-43).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 4 §Key Findings]

## Methodology

Qualitative case research. Worked directly with Hackrod, Moderna, Ubisoft, and Starbucks. Semi-structured and unstructured interviews with lead personnel in innovation and product development, supplemented by publicly available sources (p. 44). Exact interview count not specified.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 4 §Methodology]

## Relevance to Research

- **RQ1 (governance practices)**: The supervised/unsupervised sovereignty distinction (p. 30) and governance mechanisms discussion (p. 40) provide conceptual grounding for human oversight structures.
- **P2 (workflow control rigidity)**: Supervised vs. unsupervised distinction and sequencing choices (human-first vs. machine-first, Table 1, p. 36) directly relate to workflow control rigidity.
- **Theoretical stream**: Orchestration framework parallels governance challenge of coordinating human and AI agents, though framed for innovation rather than risk.

The paper is primarily about product innovation, not enterprise risk or compliance. Marginal relevance for the core literature review; useful for analogies about oversight structures.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 4 §Direct Relevance and §Validity]

## Limitations

- Does not address enterprise risk, incident frequency, financial loss, or compliance.
- Does not engage with principal-agent theory.
- Does not measure governance outcomes quantitatively.
- Does not address agentic AI (focuses on machine designers in innovation).
- Does not discuss shadow AI or governance maturity.
