# Mixed Methods Sequential Design

## What It Is

A two-phase research design where qualitative research (Phase 1) builds constructs or generates hypotheses, followed by quantitative research (Phase 2) that tests them on a larger sample. The phases are sequential, not concurrent.

## When to Use

- When validated measurement instruments do not exist for your construct (Phase 1 builds them)
- When you want both depth (understanding mechanisms) and breadth (generalizability)
- When a single method cannot answer both "how does this work?" and "how widespread is it?"

## The Two Phases

**Phase 1 (Qualitative):** Exploratory. Builds understanding.
- Methods: case study, interviews, document analysis, content analysis
- Output: themes, propositions, conceptual model, candidate measurement items
- Sample: small (3-8 cases, 15-25 interviews)

**Phase 2 (Quantitative):** Confirmatory. Tests generalizability.
- Methods: survey, archival data analysis, regression, SEM
- Output: statistical tests of propositions, validated relationships
- Sample: large (100-300+ respondents or firms)

## Worked Example: Sandip Mukhopadhyay (MDI, 2016)

**Topic**: How control mechanisms evolve in mobile service ecosystems

**Phase 1**: Multiple case studies of mobile VAS services in India. Interviews with platform owners and partners. Used Resource Dependency Theory as theoretical lens. Identified control portfolios (behavioral, outcome, social, input) and how they change over service lifecycle phases.

**Phase 2**: Online quantitative survey of broader set of industry experts. Tested whether the patterns found in cases hold more generally.

**Key design features:**
- Unit of analysis: the value network (not the individual firm)
- Theoretical lens chosen upfront: Resource Dependency Theory
- Phase 1 generated specific hypotheses that Phase 2 tested
- Mixed methods justified because: no prior quantitative study existed on mobile service controls in India

## Relevance to DBA Thesis (Org Restructuring)

Direct parallel:
- Phase 1: Archival content analysis of 6-8 tech firms' public filings. Code structural changes using Gioia method. Identify transformation patterns and archetypes.
- Phase 2: Test whether degree of structural transformation (index built from Phase 1 coding) predicts post-restructuring financial performance using publicly available financial data.

This gives Prashar his IV-DV quantitative test while maintaining the qualitative depth that Trebucq values.

## Philosophical Stance

Pragmatist. Phase 1 is interpretive/inductive. Phase 2 is post-positivist/deductive. The research question determines the method, not an overarching ontology.
