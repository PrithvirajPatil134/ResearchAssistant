## Summary

The DBA Programme's official dissertation proposal template specifies an 8-section structure within a 20-30 page constraint. It provides guidance on what each section should accomplish, from the abstract through initial references. The template emphasizes that a proposal must demonstrate the candidate has built solid domain knowledge, identified a genuine gap, and planned a feasible methodology.

[Source: DBAThesis DissertationProposalTemplate.docx, full document]

## Key Arguments

### Document Constraints

- Minimum 20 pages, maximum 30 pages.
- Abstract: no longer than 200 words.
- Keywords: up to twelve, alphabetically ordered.

[Source: DBAThesis DissertationProposalTemplate.docx, §Abstract and §Keywords]

### 8-Section Structure

#### 1. Abstract / Project Summary

Must provide "a summary of your proposed research intelligible to a reader who is not a specialist in this field; summarize the aims, significance and expected outcomes of the research." Should describe: the knowledge domain, key issues, research statement, proposed approach, expected results, and anticipated implications.

The framing advice: "imagine that you were looking for financial support from someone who is very busy" and you have two minutes to explain.

[Source: §Abstract]

#### 2. Introduction / Background

Gives an overview of the research project. Must: explain the background focusing on "major issues of its knowledge domain," present the research statement (hypothesis, research question, project statement, or goal statement), and clarify the "extent to which you expect its outcomes to represent an advance in the knowledge domain."

Must "catch the reader's interest" and cite "all relevant references pertaining to the major issues described." Closes with a brief description of subsequent chapters.

Practical advice: "Many authors prefer to postpone writing the Introduction till the rest of the document is finished."

[Source: §1- Introduction / Background]

#### 3. Literature Review / State of the Art / Foundation Works

"Usually the more extensive part of a research proposal." Must demonstrate: solid knowledge of the field, familiarity with main issues, critical identification and evaluation of key literature, and an "innovative and coherent view integrating and synthesizing the main aspects of the field."

Purpose: "give credit to the authors who laid the groundwork for your research, so that when, in the following chapter, your research objectives are further clarified, the reader is able to recognize beyond doubt that what you are attempting to do has not been done in the past."

Should be "accompanied by comprehensive references."

[Source: §2- Literature Review]

#### 4. Research Objectives

Three sub-sections:

- **a) Aims/Objectives**: "be clear on what do you want to know, to prove, demonstrate, analyze, test, investigate or examine? List your aims in a logical sequence."
- **b) Research Questions**: Include "possible hypothesis or problems to be addressed."
- **c) Relevancy and Implications**: "the reason of studying such topic/exploring such question. What is the relevancy? Why is it so important?"

[Source: §3- Research Objectives]

#### 5. Methodology

The "how to" component. Should provide "a general idea of possible methods you intend to use are adequate for the research at hand." Notes that "many topics will only have a methodology defined during the DBA" but candidates should "demonstrate that you have already given some thought about how you will do things."

Must show the project is "feasible in the time period available."

[Source: §5- Methodology]

#### 6. Outline / Work Plan

Provides "a concise outline of the work you have carried out so far and of the progress you have made toward the aims of the project." If preliminary results exist, present them here.

Includes a "general monthly/quarterly outline" of how work will be completed within the scheduled timeframe. Example format: "Year 1: Jan-Jun: In-depth readings and..."

[Source: §6- Outline /Work Plan]

#### 7. Conclusion

"Briefly restate the objectives of your research project, recap the research approach you plan to follow, and clarify in a few words what you expect to find out, why it is scientifically valuable to find it out, and on what basis you expect to evaluate the validity of your results."

Should reinforce "the conviction that your approach is solidly oriented toward results, that the topic is timely and relevant, and that the outcomes of the project will contribute significantly to the enhancement of the field."

[Source: §7- Conclusion]

#### 8. Initial References

All references cited throughout the proposal, following field-specific citation conventions. Template provides format examples for journal articles, books, book chapters, and conference proceedings (APA-adjacent style).

[Source: §8- Initial References]

The proposal template's structural guidance applies to the current DBA thesis: organizational restructuring triggered by GenAI adoption in large enterprises, examined through punctuated equilibrium theory (Tushman and Romanelli). The method is archival quantitative, using SEC 10-K filings and TMT composition data from 6-8 US-listed enterprises. ## Limitations

- Institutional template only; does not prescribe specific research standards, quality thresholds, or paradigm preferences.
- Generic across all DBA research areas; does not address IS-specific or AI-specific methodological considerations.
- Does not specify word counts per section beyond the overall 20-30 page constraint and 200-word abstract limit.
