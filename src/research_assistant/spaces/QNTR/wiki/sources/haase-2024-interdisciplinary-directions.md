## Summary

Argues that understanding the effects of process automation (RPA and LLMs) on business processes requires an interdisciplinary lens integrating three perspectives: automation technology (IS/BPM), human-automation interaction (HAI from psychology), and routine dynamics (organizational studies) (p. 580). Automation does not simply replace human activity but "changes it in ways often unintended and unanticipated" (p. 585).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 6 §Core Argument]

## Key Arguments

### Three Disciplinary Perspectives

1. **IS/BPM (automation technology)**: RPA effectiveness depends on human expert availability and skills (p. 581). Automation leads to skill loss if humans do not understand underlying reasoning (p. 581).
2. **Human-Automation Interaction (HAI)**: Three key risks: automation bias (using automation as heuristic replacement), complacency (insufficient monitoring), and skill degradation (pp. 584-585).
3. **Routine dynamics**: Process automation transforms organizational routines, going beyond initial adoption to reshape workflow dynamics (p. 587).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 6 §Key Findings]

### Degree-of-Automation Tradeoff

"Higher degrees of automation yield better performance under normal conditions but worse performance during failures due to increased complacency and skill degradation" (p. 585). This is the core empirical finding from HAI research supporting an inverted U-shape.

"An appropriate function allocation can only meet certain aspects, as higher degrees of automation are always accompanied by positive and negative aspects" (p. 585).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 6 §Key Findings]

### Skill Loss Example

A new employee unable to execute a process when a bot fails because "she's never done that" (p. 581). Demonstrates concrete risk when automation replaces without transferring process knowledge.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 6 §Key Findings]

### Countermeasures

Training on automation fallibility and adaptive automation reduce automation bias (p. 586). This supports the logic that governance practices (training, monitoring protocols) reduce incidents.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 6 §Key Findings]

### Research Directions (Figure 1, p. 588)

Proposed: user-centric design for task automation, understanding individual differences, long-term psychological impact, adaptive tools and training, AI integration in routine tasks, dynamics in teams with AI-based members.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 6 §Key Findings]

## Methodology

Panel report based on a discussion at the Routines.Research.Community workshop, March 2023, Berlin. Integrates literature review across three disciplines with panel discussion insights. Appendix A contains edited transcript. No primary empirical data collection.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 6 §Methodology]

## Relevance to Research

- **RQ1 (risk categories)**: Identifies risk categories from automation: automation bias, complacency, skill degradation, loss of situation awareness, automation surprises (pp. 584-585).
- **P1 (governance maturity reduces incidents)**: Countermeasures (training, adaptive automation) reducing automation bias supports the governance-reduces-risk logic.
- **P2 (workflow control rigidity)**: The degree-of-automation tradeoff directly supports the workflow control rigidity construct.
- **P4 (inverted U-shape)**: Provides empirical support from HAI research for the proposition that optimal performance requires a balanced automation level.
- **Theoretical stream**: Routine dynamics perspective connects to IT governance-to-business-value lineage (how automation transforms organizational processes).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 6 §Direct Relevance]

## Limitations

- Does not address agentic AI specifically (focuses on RPA and LLMs in process automation).
- Does not provide firm-level quantitative data on risk outcomes.
- Does not engage with principal-agent theory.
- Does not test governance maturity or shadow AI as variables.
- Panel report format limits depth of empirical evidence.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 6 §What It Does NOT Do]
