## Key Framework Taught

### Deductive vs. Inductive Approach

Barneto presents the field-research relationship through the deductive/inductive continuum, citing Kalika (Research Methods for the DBA, pp.39-51):

- **Deductive (hypothetico-deductive)**: Start with literature, formulate hypotheses, go into field to confirm or disconfirm
- **Inductive**: Start in field, observe facts, compare to literature, conceptualize, generalize, propose new theory
- **Iterative in practice**: "Management researchers often go back and forth between theories and observations during their research process and combine the inductive and deductive approaches"

[Source: `Research Methodology.pdf`, pp.22-23]

The user's archival quantitative design (deductive: governance maturity literature → hypotheses → regulatory/event data) is directly aligned with the hypothetico-deductive path described in this framework.

### Topic Selection Factors

Five factors for manager-researchers when choosing a topic: objectives, resources and competences, opportunities presented by the environment, feasibility, and the thesis supervisor's opinion.
[Source: `Research Methodology.pdf`, p.26]

### Demonstrating Contribution

Barneto's framework for demonstrating thesis contribution (p.21):
1. Summarize the initial research question
2. For each aspect of contribution, state what was already known
3. Summarize how thesis results add to what was known
4. Show why this matters from a managerial and/or theoretical perspective

"The literature review is also fundamental because a strong literature review will help you demonstrate the contribution of your thesis. Your contribution is what you add to existing knowledge."
[Source: `Research Methodology.pdf`, p.21]

### Research Problem Definition

Citing Josserand (Research Methods for the DBA, pp.79-94): "There is a problem when you feel the need to bridge the gap between an unsatisfactory starting situation and a desirable ending situation." A general research problem can emerge from situations in business organizations. Then it must be transformed into a precisely problematized research topic by appropriating notions and concepts.
[Source: `Research Methodology.pdf`, p.28]

### Research Output Formats

Barneto's matrix distinguishing audiences (Researchers vs. Managers) and media (Research Article, Managerial Article, Oral Communication, Book, Report). Research articles have very strong researcher audience reach and weak manager reach; books range from moderate to strong for both.
[Source: `Research Methodology.pdf`, p.16]

### Journal Ranking System

A four-level categorization used in the course: journals divided into 4 categories (1-4) in decreasing quality order, with 1* for generalist journals of category 1 appearing "particularly remarkable" (e.g., Management Science).
[Source: `Research Methodology.pdf`, p.17]

### AI Use Policy

Acknowledges the use of generative AI tools (OpenAI, Gemini) for questionnaire creation and other materials, but cautions: "be careful to ensure that AI remains a tool to help you and not an end in itself."
[Source: `Research Methodology.pdf`, p.20]

## DBA Thesis Expectations

Barneto's slides cite Paul Beaulieu's seven features of a DBA thesis:
1. Intended to produce scientific knowledge meeting needs of management communities and practice
2. Must produce new scientific knowledge; requires systematic review of available knowledge and rigorous demonstration of the need for further knowledge
3. Must allow for practice development and reflective critical debate
4. Must develop scientific communication skills (written and oral)
5. "Naturally" oriented toward management fieldwork; researcher should spend substantial time in practice settings; pragmatic methodological orientation
6. Should demonstrate mastery of scientific production and communication processes; methodological rigor is essential
7. Ideal for learning about debates in scientific communities and developing knowledge transfer skills

[Source: `Research Methodology.pdf`, pp.12-14]
