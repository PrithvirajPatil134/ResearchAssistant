Dear Prof. Prashar,

I hope this message finds you well. I am writing to share the current draft of my QNTR term paper for your initial review before formal submission. The paper is titled "Agentic AI Governance and Its Impact on Enterprise Risk and Business Performance."

The draft applies the research methodology concepts from the course to a topic I am developing for my broader doctoral research. It proposes an exploratory sequential mixed-methods design examining whether agentic AI governance maturity predicts firm-level risk outcomes. The theoretical lens combines principal-agent theory (Kolt, 2025) with the IT governance-to-business-value lineage (Weill and Ross, 2004; Mikalef et al., 2021, 2025). The empirical anchor on the BPM side is Vu et al. (2025), whose qualitative study of 22 practitioners is the closest existing work but stops at perceptions without outcome measurement.

A few points I would particularly value your feedback on:

1. **P4 (inverted U-shape).** I added a fourth proposition arguing that the relationship between workflow control rigidity and risk is non-linear. This emerged from the in-class assignment discussion where you noted the importance of focusing on governance processes. The logic is that both insufficient and excessive control increase risk, with moderate control being optimal. I am uncertain whether this overextends the model given the sample size constraints of Phase 2 (40 to 50 firms).

2. **Theory-in-use lens in Phase 1.** Following your suggestion to explore theory-in-use, I have incorporated an Argyris and Schon (1974) lens into the Phase 1 axial coding stage to distinguish between espoused governance (what firms say in public forums) and governance-in-use (what incident data and practitioner accounts reveal). I would appreciate your view on whether this is well placed within Phase 1 or whether it warrants a separate treatment.

3. **Sample size and statistical power.** Phase 2 targets 40 to 50 firms with binary logistic regression, an interaction term (P3), and a quadratic term (P4). I have flagged this as a limitation but would welcome your assessment of whether the analytical ambition is realistic for this sample.

The full draft is attached. I look forward to your feedback.
