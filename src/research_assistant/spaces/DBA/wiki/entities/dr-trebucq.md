## Key Guidance (from May 1 response)

### Definitional Clarity

Wants to better understand: what is meant by "governance" of agentic AI systems? Are there real-life examples?
[Source: `spaces/DBA/feedback/response_dr_trebucq_research_direction_20260501.md`, Message 1, para 2]

### Executive Mindset Reproduction

Frames agentic AI systems as reproducing "previous mindsets of top executives." Questions whether this is enough to reduce risk and add value to risk reduction. Distinguishes risk detection from the many subsequent steps until risk reduction.
[Source: `spaces/DBA/feedback/response_dr_trebucq_research_direction_20260501.md`, Message 1, para 3]

### Autonomy as Double-Edged

How can an autonomous agent communicate with other systems? Autonomy is both advantage and limit. Can the system learn from its failures and mistakes?
[Source: `spaces/DBA/feedback/response_dr_trebucq_research_direction_20260501.md`, Message 1, paras 4-5]

### Alternative Theoretical Frameworks

- Agency theory links to financial performance through agency costs (the standard path)
- Hidden costs theory (Savall, Zardet) with socio-economic management theory as alternative
- Decision theory and cognitive theories relevant when discussing AI
- Cognitive corporate governance approach

[Source: `spaces/DBA/feedback/response_dr_trebucq_research_direction_20260501.md`, Message 1, paras 6-7]

### Inductive Qualitative Research

Qualitative research can be inductive and does not need previous theory, "because we want to explore something new."
