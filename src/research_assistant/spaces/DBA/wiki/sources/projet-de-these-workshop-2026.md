## Proposal Structure Guidance

The deck specifies the following structure for a thesis proposal, in this order: [p.2]

1. **Title** — "What is this about and why should I read it?"
2. **Abstract** — "What is this all about? In a summary…."
3. **Introduction** — Why is it relevant to do this research? What would be the significance and Contribution? Discuss how your study contributes to the existing body of knowledge. Highlight the expected impact of your research on the field.
4. **Literature Review** — Who have studied this subject before? Identify gaps, debates, or areas where further research is needed. Establish the theoretical framework that informs your research.
5. **Objective and Research Questions** — What is your research about? What are the research question(s)? Why is it relevant?
6. **Methods** — How are you going to do it? Describe the research design and methods. Explain data collection methods, tools, and procedures. Discuss the sampling strategy and justify its appropriateness. Provide details on data analysis techniques.
7. **Planning / Research Timeline** — How and when do you plan to organize yourself? Outline a timeline. Include milestones, key activities, and deadlines.
8. **Results and Conclusion** — What are the possible outcomes, study limitations and implications? Briefly outline expected outcomes and results. Summarize key points. Emphasize the significance of your research.
9. **References** — What are the scientific references? Provide a list of all sources cited.

## Research Objective Formulation

The workshop provides a step-by-step method for writing research objectives: [p.6–p.9]

- **Key principle**: "You don't do a research to PROVE something. You should remain NEUTRAL and try to find if something is true or not." [p.6]
- **Step 1**: "I would like to find out about…." [p.6]
- **Step 2**: Transform Step 1 into a formal objective sentence beginning with "The aim of this research is to…" [p.7]

A structured formula is provided: [p.9]

(A) Pick the verb: analyze, assess, compare, determine, examine, evaluate, explore, identify, investigate, propose

(B) Pick the complement: the impact of, the effect(s) of, the performance of, the implications of, the relationship of/between, the factors, the adoption, the preferences, the drivers, the practices/a framework, the reason(s)

(C) Specify what…

Worked examples are provided for each verb (investigate, analyze, examine, explore, assess, determine, identify, compare, propose, discuss, evaluate) with two example objective sentences each. [p.8–p.9]
