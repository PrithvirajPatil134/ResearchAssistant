## Summary

Proposes a framework for how AI changes open innovation (OI) through three mechanisms: enhancing established OI practices, enabling new forms of OI, and replacing some traditional OI practices (p. 5, Table 1). Multi-agent systems offer novel approaches to distributed problem-solving, but the paper's focus is innovation, not governance or risk.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 3 §Core Argument]

## Key Arguments

### AI's Three Effects on Open Innovation

1. **Enhances OI**: innovation search (NLP, sentiment analysis), partner search, idea evaluation (AI better at screening bad ideas than selecting best, p. 10), resource utilization (pp. 9-11).
2. **Enables new OI forms**: AI-enabled markets, new business models, federated learning for privacy-preserving collaboration (pp. 11-12).
3. **Replaces OI**: AI ideation (ideas rated more creative than laypeople's, p. 13), synthetic data replacing data sharing, multi-agent systems replacing coordination-heavy OI (pp. 13-15).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 3 §Key Findings]

### Multi-Agent Systems

Can "operate autonomously and interact on the basis of predefined rules and protocols" to address OI coordination challenges (p. 10). The barrier to implementing multi-agent systems "through multiple autonomous chatbots, is now lower than ever" (p. 10).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 3 §Key Findings]

## Methodology

Conceptual framework paper with illustrative examples. No empirical data collection. Typology constructed from OI literature and recent AI developments (Table 1, p. 9).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 3 §Methodology]

## Relevance to Research

**Tangential relevance only.** The paper's focus is open innovation, not governance or risk management.

- The multi-agent systems discussion (pp. 10-11) touches on coordination protocols and predefined rules, which are governance-adjacent but framed as innovation mechanisms rather than risk controls.
- The low barrier to implementing multi-agent chatbots (p. 10) indirectly supports the shadow AI concern (P3).
- Does not address enterprise risk, governance maturity, incident frequency, or compliance.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 3 §Direct Relevance]

## Limitations

- Does not address AI governance, risk management, or compliance.
- Does not discuss enterprise risk outcomes.
- Does not engage with principal-agent theory or IT governance frameworks.
- Does not provide empirical data on governance practices or their effects.
- Does not address agentic AI governance specifically.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 3 §What It Does NOT Do]
