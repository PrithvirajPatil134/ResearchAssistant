## Summary

Rehman and Alharthi provide an introductory overview of three research paradigms (positivism, interpretivism, critical theory) by examining each through four components: ontology, epistemology, methodology, and methods. The paper concludes that researchers should not treat paradigms as mutually exclusive religions but should choose approaches guided by the requirements of their study.

[Source: paradigms_20260414.pdf, pp. 51-59]

## Key Arguments

### Four Components of a Research Paradigm

1. **Ontology**: "the nature of our beliefs about reality" (p. 51, citing Richards 2003: 33). The ontological question asks: does a "singular, verifiable reality and truth" exist, or are there "socially constructed multiple realities"? (p. 51, citing Patton 2002: 134).

2. **Epistemology**: "the branch of philosophy that studies the nature of knowledge and the process by which knowledge is acquired and validated" (p. 52, citing Gall, Gall & Borg 2003: 13). Leads researchers to debate "the possibility and desirability of objectivity, subjectivity, causality, validity, generalisability" (p. 52, citing Patton 2002: 134).

3. **Methodology**: "an articulated, theoretically informed approach to the production of data" (p. 52, citing Ellen 1984: 9). The "strategy, plan of action, process or design" informing choice of methods (p. 52, citing Crotty 1998: 3).

4. **Methods**: "specific means of collecting and analysing data, such as questionnaires and open ended interviews" (p. 52). Use of particular methods does not entail specific ontological or epistemological assumptions.

[Source: pp. 51-52]

### Three Paradigms Compared

#### Positivism

- **Ontology**: Realism. Reality exists independently of humans, governed by immutable laws (p. 53).
- **Epistemology**: Objectivism. Researchers observe phenomena without affecting them (p. 53).
- **Methodology**: Deductive. Hypotheses proposed, then confirmed or rejected via statistical analysis. Purpose: "measure, control, predict, construct laws and ascribe causality" (p. 54, citing Cohen et al. 2007).
- **Methods**: True experiments, quasi-experiments, standardized tests, surveys with closed-ended questionnaires (p. 55).
- **Quality criteria**: Internal validity, external validity, reliability, objectivity (p. 55, citing Guba & Lincoln 1994).
- **Post-positivism** (critical realism): acknowledges reality exists independently but "can only be apprehended imperfectly because of the complexity of social phenomena" (p. 54).

[Source: pp. 53-55]

#### Interpretivism

- **Ontology**: Anti-foundationalist. Rejects a single verifiable reality. "Truth and reality are created, not discovered" (p. 55).
- **Epistemology**: Subjective. "External reality cannot be directly accessible to observers without being contaminated by their worldviews, concepts, backgrounds" (p. 55).
- **Methodology**: Inductive. Researcher discovers patterns in data, collapses them under broad themes. Goal is to understand social phenomena "through the eyes of the participants rather than the researcher" (p. 56, citing Cohen et al. 2007: 21).
- **Methods**: Open-ended interviews (standardized, semi-standardized, informal conversational), observations, field notes, documents (p. 56).
- **Quality criteria**: Credibility (internal validity), transferability (external validity), dependability (reliability), confirmability (objectivity) (p. 56, citing Guba & Lincoln 1994: 114).

[Source: pp. 55-57]

#### Critical Theory

- **Ontology**: Historical realism. Reality exists but "has been shaped by cultural, political, ethnic, gender and religious factors" (p. 57).
- **Epistemology**: Subjective. "No object can be researched without being affected by the researcher" (p. 57).
- **Methodology**: Dialogic and dialectical. Aims to bring change in participants' outlook on oppressive social systems (p. 58, citing Guba & Lincoln 1994). Uses collaborative approaches.
- **Methods**: Critical ethnography, critical discourse analysis, action research, ideology critique (p. 58).
- **Quality criteria**: Degree to which subjects' misapprehensions about dominant ideology are exposed and the degree to which research facilitates "action designed to redress the unequal and oppressive structures" (p. 58, citing Richards 2003: 40).

[Source: pp. 57-58]

### Conclusion on Paradigm Choice

The authors reject viewing paradigms as "three religions" where "adherence to one paradigm makes one a heretic according to the lenses of the adherents of other paradigms" (p. 58). The decision should be "guided by the necessities and requirements of a research study rather than the obdurate insistence of adhering to one particular philosophical outlook to the exclusion of others" (p. 51, Abstract).

[Source: pp. 51, 58]

## Methodology

Literature review and conceptual synthesis. No empirical data collection. Draws on foundational methodological texts (Guba & Lincoln 1994; Grix 2004; Richards 2003; Patton 2002; Cohen et al. 2007).

[Source: pp. 51-59]

## Relevance to Research

For the agentic AI governance research:

- Provides the philosophical vocabulary for the methodology chapter: ontology, epistemology, methodology, methods.
- The exploratory sequential mixed-methods design occupies a pragmatist position (not covered in this paper but implicitly supported by the conclusion that paradigm choice should follow research needs rather than ideological commitments).
- Phase 1 (qualitative, inductive, theory-building from cases) aligns with interpretivist assumptions about knowledge construction from executive discourse.
- Phase 2 (quantitative, deductive, hypothesis testing via survey) aligns with post-positivist assumptions about testing relationships.
- The paper's quality criteria framework (different criteria per paradigm) informs how to evaluate each phase using its appropriate standards.

[Source: pp. 51-59]

## Limitations

- Introductory treatment; does not cover pragmatism, which is the philosophical foundation for mixed-methods research.
- Published in a non-ranked journal (International Journal of Educational Investigations); used here as a teaching reference rather than a primary scholarly source.
- Does not address how paradigms interact in mixed-methods designs or the "paradigm wars" resolution through pragmatism.
- Focused on educational research; organizational and IS research may have additional paradigmatic considerations.

[Source: pp. 51-59]

## Key Quotes

- "Ontology and epistemology are to research what 'footings' are to a house: they form the foundations of the whole edifice." (p. 51, citing Grix 2004: 59)
- "One should choose methods and methodology which one finds most suitable for investigating the phenomena one sets out to investigate." (p. 58)
- "Positivism assumes that reality exists independently of humans. It is not mediated by our senses and it is governed by immutable laws." (p. 53)
- "Interpretivism rejects the notion that a single, verifiable reality exists independent of our senses." (p. 55)
- "The aim of critical educational research is not merely to explain or understand society but to change it." (p. 57, citing Patton 2002)
