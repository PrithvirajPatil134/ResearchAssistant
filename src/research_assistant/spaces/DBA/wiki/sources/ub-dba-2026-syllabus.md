# UB DBA 2026 Doctoral Seminars Syllabus

## Programme Overview

The DBA is described as "comprised by a series of Doctoral Seminars in which each topic is aimed to expand the doctoral student research's view aiming also to incorporate the different types of knowledge in the managerial and business field into the student's doctoral research" [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, p.1].

All seminars are evaluated via "ongoing continuous assessment / written work with reflection on one's doctoral thesis" and the committee of doctoral professors performs an overall evaluation [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, p.1].

Two distinct assessment structures are specified:

**For the first year:**
1. Research paper
2. Grand Oral
3. Oral Defense

**For the final years:**
1. Research Practice
2. Dissertation
3. Oral Defense

[Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, p.1]

The programme is organised into two teaching units (UEs): UE1 — Principles of Business Research (eight courses) and UE2 — Epistemology and Methodology (three courses) [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.2–33].

## UE1 Courses: Principles of Business Research

All UE1 courses carry 20 Doctoral Credits Points each [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.2–24].

**Course 1: Sustainability Finance & Financial Markets**
- Instructor: Dr. Alberto Arbulu
- Credits: 20 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include sustainable finance principles, ESG criteria and rating agencies, sustainable investment strategies, the role of financial institutions, stock exchanges and sustainability indices, regulatory frameworks (EU taxonomy, TCFD), and corporate sustainability reporting standards (GRI, SASB) [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.2–4]

**Course 2: Financial Information, Accounting Reporting, and Control in Corporate Governance**
- Instructor: Dr. Frédérique Bardinet
- Credits: 20 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include corporate governance theoretical frameworks (agency theory, stewardship theory), financial reporting standards (IFRS, GAAP), disclosure requirements, audit and assurance, board structure and executive compensation, shareholder activism, CSR reporting, and ethical issues in financial reporting [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.5–6]

**Course 3: Corporate Governance**
- Instructor: Dr. Jean-Pierre Pichard
- Credits: 20 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include key theories and frameworks in corporate governance, the role of boards of directors, executive compensation structures, shareholder rights and activism, regulatory frameworks (Sarbanes-Oxley Act, Dodd-Frank Act), governance in family-owned businesses and multinational corporations, ethical considerations including CSR, and the relationship between governance practices and firm performance [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.7–9]

**Course 4: Management of Business Models, Entrepreneurship, and Innovation**
- Instructor: Dr. Franck Duquesnoy
- Credits: 20 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include Business Model Canvas, value creation and capture, transaction cost economics, entrepreneurial mindset, lean startup methodology, open innovation, value proposition design, customer development, minimum viable product (MVP), disruptive innovation, scaling new ventures, corporate entrepreneurship, and digital transformation [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.10–12]

**Course 5: Intercultural Project Management**
- Instructor: Dr. Emmanuelle Sauvage
- Credits: 20 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include Hofstede's cultural dimensions theory, Trompenaars' model of national culture differences, the GLOBE project, intercultural communication strategies, cross-cultural leadership styles, managing intercultural teams, conflict resolution in multicultural teams, stakeholder management in intercultural projects, virtual teams and digital collaboration tools, and ethics and CSR in a global context [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.13–15]

**Course 6: Human Resources and Organizational Behavior**
- Instructor: Dr. Olivier Herrbach
- Credits: 20 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include talent management, motivation theories (Maslow, Herzberg, Deci & Ryan), employee engagement, leadership theories (transformational, transactional, servant leadership), organizational culture, diversity and inclusion, theories of organizational change (Lewin, Kotter), work-life balance, well-being, and technology in HR including HR analytics and AI in HR [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.16–18]

**Course 7: Doctoral Seminar in International Business Economics**
- Instructor: Dr. Jan Schaaper
- Credits: 20 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include international trade patterns and theories, trade policies and negotiations, exchange rates and monetary policy, international capital flows, multinational corporations (MNCs) and global strategies, entry modes and location strategies, foreign direct investment, global value chains, offshoring and outsourcing, and digital globalization [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.19–21]

**Course 8: Marketing and Commerce in an International Context**
- Instructor: Dr. Claudia Rusu
- Credits: 20 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include global marketing strategies, Hofstede's cultural dimensions applied to marketing, international market entry strategies (exporting, licensing, franchising, joint ventures, wholly owned subsidiaries), cross-cultural consumer behaviour, global branding and positioning, digital transformation in international marketing, e-commerce, data analytics, and ethical and sustainable international marketing [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.22–24]

## UE2 Courses: Epistemology and Methodology

**Course 1: Introduction to Management Research**
- Instructor: Dr. Pascal Barneto
- Credits: 20 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include research philosophies (positivism, interpretivism, pragmatism), inductive and deductive approaches, mixed-methods research, formulating research questions and hypotheses, systematic literature review techniques, qualitative research methods (case studies, ethnography, grounded theory), quantitative data analysis (descriptive and inferential statistics, regression, hypothesis testing), qualitative data analysis (coding, thematic analysis), and ethical considerations in research
- Statistical software referenced: SPSS, Stata [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, p.26]
- Qualitative analysis software referenced: NVivo [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, p.26]
[Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.25–27]

**Course 2: Quantitative Methods Applied to Management Science**
- Instructors: Dr. Jean-Philippe Galan and Dr. Catherine Viot
- Credits: 40 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include statistical methods for management (descriptive statistics, probability distributions, inferential statistics, hypothesis testing), regression analysis (simple and multiple regression), multivariate analysis (factor analysis, cluster analysis, discriminant analysis), time series analysis and forecasting (ARIMA models), optimization techniques (linear programming, integer programming), decision analysis (decision trees, utility theory, risk analysis), simulation methods (Monte Carlo, discrete-event), data envelopment analysis (DEA), and big data analytics and machine learning (supervised and unsupervised learning, predictive analytics)
- Software referenced: R, Python, Excel, SAS [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, p.28]
[Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.28–30]

**Course 3: Qualitative Methods Applied to Management Science**
- Instructors: Dr. Vincent Maymo and Dr. Stéphane Trébucq
- Credits: 40 Doctoral Credits Points
- Topics include research philosophies (interpretivism, constructivism, critical theory), grounded theory, ethnography, phenomenology, action research, case study research, in-depth interview design and facilitation, focus groups, participant and non-participant observation, documentary data analysis, qualitative data coding and thematic analysis, narrative analysis, and writing qualitative research reports
- Qualitative analysis software referenced: NVivo [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, p.31]
[Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.31–33]

## Grading System

"The University of Bordeaux, being a Public French University, uses the French Grading System from 0 to 20. Below 10/20 is considered as FAIL." [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, p.1]

Assessment types across all courses are described as "ongoing continuous assessment / written work with reflection on one's doctoral thesis" [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, p.1]. Each course concludes with a session described as "Student Presentations and Research Papers" covering presentation of research findings, peer feedback and discussion, and submission of final research papers or proposals [Syllabus UB DBA 2026.pdf, pp.4, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30, 33].
