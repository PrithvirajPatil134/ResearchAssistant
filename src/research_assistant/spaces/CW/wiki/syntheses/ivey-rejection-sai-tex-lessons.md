## What Happened

Ivey Publishing rejected the case "SAI TEX LTD.: A RED QUEEN'S RACE" by
Prithviraj Patil and Kakoli Sen. The decision was a reject, not a
revise-and-resubmit. Ivey's wording was final: "we will be unable to publish
it" and "we are unable to register this particular case"
[communication/ivey-rejection-sai-tex-2026.md].

## The Two Reasons Ivey Gave

Ivey gave two distinct criticisms [communication/ivey-rejection-sai-tex-2026.md]:

1. **Orientation**: the submission "reads as a narrative portrait of the
   protagonist rather than as a decision case for a student."

2. **Analysis displacement**: "the case performs the analysis it should be
   asking students to undertake. The Teaching Note reproduces this orientation,
   delivering worked solutions in expository voice rather than facilitation
   guidance."

## How Each Reason Traces to the Documents

**Reason 1 (narrative portrait).** The case opening is written as literary
scene-setting: the humid April night, looms at 300 RPM, the phone buzzing with
an interest debit and a WhatsApp message, the wife's quote from the previous
evening [output/Sai_Tex_Ltd_Case_Study_v7_PUBLICATION.md]. The case also
resolves its own tension rather than leaving it open: "The question was no
longer whether to change, but which change would not destroy everything he had
built" [output/Sai_Tex_Ltd_Case_Study_v7_PUBLICATION.md]. A decision case
should end at the dilemma, not narrate past it.

**Reason 2 (analysis displacement).** The teaching note answers its own
questions in expository voice instead of guiding discussion. Examples:
"The answer I would give: technology is the root constraint" and the fully
worked break-even concluding "Sai Tex's problem is not cyclical"
[output/Teaching_Note_v8_revised.md]. The option matrix is pre-scored with a
recommended reading rather than a facilitation plan
[output/Teaching_Note_v8_revised.md].

**A third tell Ivey did not name but is present.** The case names its own theory.
The title is "A Red Queen's Race," and the teaching note builds the analysis on
the Red Queen Effect. The workspace's own Publication Playbook flags this as a
violation of published-case convention: cases keep theory implicit, "12/14
implicit, 0/14 textbook-style" [knowledge/Publication_Playbook_v4.md].

## Root-Cause Reading

The two reasons are one underlying problem. The case did the analytical work, so
the teaching note had no discussion left to facilitate. Fixing this means
reallocation: pull analysis out of the case and end it at the dilemma; convert
the teaching note from worked answers to facilitation guidance with an
instructor answer key.

## Cross-Publisher Implications

Not both criticisms travel to other publishers equally.

- **The decision-case requirement is Ivey-specific.** The CASE Journal (Emerald)
  states "Cases do not have to have a decision focus; illustrative, descriptive,
  and analytical cases are welcome"
  [web: emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/tcj, accessed 2026-06-11]. Its editor
  reinforced that TCJ welcomes "evaluative, illustrative, descriptive or
  analytical cases and critical incidents"
  [web: emerald.com TCJ editor letter, accessed 2026-06-11]. So Reason 1 would
  not automatically sink the case at TCJ.

- **The teaching-note facilitation standard likely travels.** TCJ still requires
  a teaching note with "discussion questions and suggested responses and
  teaching strategies"
  [web: emeraldgrouppublishing.com/tcj.htm, accessed 2026-06-11]. A teaching note
  that hands over conclusions rather than facilitating is a weakness most case
  journals' reviewers would flag. So Reason 2's fix is needed for any publisher.

## Important Context: Primary vs Secondary Case

The publisher shortlist in the user's thread with Prof. Sen (Business Case
Journal, The CASE Journal, IJCSM at HEC Montreal) arose specifically around
cases built on *secondary* data
[communication/Case Writing Topic Guidance.eml]. Sai Tex is a *primary*,
consent-based case: the factory owner verbally agreed to participate, with a
consent form to be drafted [knowledge/Case_Idea_Condensed_Ichalkaranji.md]. The
consent basis widens publisher options rather than narrowing them.
