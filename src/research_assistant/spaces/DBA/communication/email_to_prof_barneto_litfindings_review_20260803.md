Dear Professor Barneto,

I have shared the literature findings from our June discussion with you as a Google Doc. My apologies for sending these a little later than the July timing we discussed; I wanted the survey to rest on full-text readings of the anchor papers rather than abstracts, and tracking down the paywalled ones took some time.

The memo does what you asked for. It states a single research question with three sub-questions, names punctuated equilibrium as the anchoring theory with disruption theory as a supporting lens, and works through the literature as what we know, what we do not yet know, and how the theory can be tested against it. It is organized around the three things the study has to measure: how intensively a firm is adopting generative AI, whether a firm's transformation over a given period is revolutionary or gradual, and the organizational structure whose change that coding turns on. For each, I set out the method the literature already provides and where the evidence runs thin for the generative-AI period specifically.

I have also identified the spine papers, the short list that anchors the design, and noted for each what it fixes. As you suggested, the design leans on publicly available data throughout: SEC filings, proxy statements, and earnings-call disclosures, with no primary data collection at this stage.

Given the time difference between us, the most efficient way to work through this may be for you to leave your feedback as comments directly on the Google Doc. I can see and respond to each point in context, and we can resolve most of it asynchronously without needing to align our calendars. I would of course still welcome a call for anything better discussed live.

One question I would value your guidance on. Because this is a fast-moving area, relevant papers keep appearing, and I expect more over the coming months. I would like to agree with you on how the review should handle work published after it is drafted, whether through a stated cut-off, a rolling update before the December defense, or an addendum.

Thank you for your time in reviewing this. I look forward to your comments.
