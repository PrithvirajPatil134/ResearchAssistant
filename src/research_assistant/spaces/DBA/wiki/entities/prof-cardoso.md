## Key Guidance

### Causal Terminology Hierarchy

Careful about language implying causation beyond what the design supports:
- Correlation < Influence < Impact < Moderation
- Using "how" in an objective already assumes a relationship exists; "if" is more neutral
- Any assumptions must be supported by the literature review

[Source: `spaces/DBA/knowledge/Prof Cardasso Class Notes.docx`, paragraph: "By stating 'how' in the beginning, you are already assuming that governance mechanisms moderates the relationship... Replacing 'if' with 'how' has a more neutral take"]

### Research Objective Construction

- Should start with "The aim of this research is to..." or "The objective of this research is to..."
- Should have business context for professors to find it relevant
- Think on the lines of "I would like to find out about..." to narrow the topic
- Avoid broad topics and terminologies

[Source: `spaces/DBA/knowledge/Prof Cardasso Class Notes.docx`, under "Objective & Research Question"]

### Title Construction

- General statement + ":" or "?" + Specific focus (who, where, when, how, why)
- Using a question mark in the title increases reader interest
- Title and objective should complement each other and be coherent
- Have multiple title options and narrow down based on how the objective is phrased

[Source: `spaces/DBA/knowledge/Prof Cardasso Class Notes.docx`, under "Title" and "Title Contd."]

### Structural Requirements

- 30-page limit is strict; French professors will only read until page 30 and grade based on those pages alone
- Strictly follow the structure provided by the professor
- Refer to 'Projet de These' presentation for structure
- Relevance section: max 3-5 sentences, general to specific, should read like a story

[Source: `spaces/DBA/knowledge/Prof Cardasso Class Notes.docx`, paragraphs 2-4 and "Relevancy" section]
