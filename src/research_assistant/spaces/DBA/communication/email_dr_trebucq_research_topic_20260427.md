I wanted to ask whether you might be interested in the topic I am developing for my doctoral research.

The research examines the governance of agentic AI systems and its relationship to enterprise risk. Agentic AI refers to autonomous, goal-directed software programs that plan, select tools, and take actions that commit the firm to financial and operational consequences. Unlike predictive models that output a score for a human to act on, these systems act on behalf of the firm with a degree of autonomy that creates a principal-agent problem between the organisation and its AI delegate.

The core question is whether firms that adopt more mature governance practices for agentic AI experience fewer reportable incidents, lower financial losses, and fewer compliance failures. The theoretical lens combines principal-agent theory applied to AI agents (Kolt, 2025) with the established IT governance-to-business-value lineage (Weill and Ross, 2004) and responsible AI governance frameworks (Mikalef et al., 2025). The study proposes an exploratory sequential mixed-methods design: a qualitative phase that builds governance constructs from executive discourse, regulatory filings, and practitioner interviews, followed by a quantitative phase that tests those constructs on a firm-level survey using binary logistic regression.

The topic sits at the intersection of IT governance, AI ethics, and organisational risk management. I believe it connects to your expertise in management and AI ethics, and I would welcome the opportunity to discuss it further if it is of interest to you.

I would be happy to share the working paper or set up a virtual meeting at your convenience.
