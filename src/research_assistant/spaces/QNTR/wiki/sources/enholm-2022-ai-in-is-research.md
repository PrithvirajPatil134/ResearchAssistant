## Summary

This paper conducts a systematic literature review of AI research in Information Systems between 2005 and 2020. From 1,877 initial studies, 98 were identified as primary studies through a rigorous filtering process. The paper identifies how AI is defined in IS, categorizes contributions, maps AI types and business value, and develops a research agenda. A central finding is that the "dark side" of AI (governance, risk, societal impacts) is substantially understudied in IS research, with the field disproportionately focused on machine learning and process automation applications.

## Key Arguments

1. **Lack of cumulative knowledge building**: The paper warns that "research on AI could experience a lack of cumulative building of knowledge, which has overshadowed IS research previously" (p. 1). This resonates with the concern of "fragmented adhocracy" that has plagued IS research (p. 10, citing Banville and Landry, 1989).

2. **Definitional incoherence**: Of 98 primary studies, 54 provided no clear definition of AI, and 7 defined it without citing any source (p. 6). The most robust definition for IS contexts comes from Rai et al. (2019): "the ability of a machine to perform cognitive functions that we associate with human minds, such as perceiving, reasoning, learning, interacting with the environment, problem solving, decision-making, and even demonstrating creativity" (p. 10).

3. **Machine learning dominance**: 69 of 98 primary studies focused on machine learning, with expert systems (11), NLP (6), machine vision (5), and robotics (3) receiving far less attention (p. 9). This creates gaps in understanding governance implications of non-ML AI types.

4. **Process automation bias**: The most common business value reported was process automation (49 studies), followed by cognitive insight (32) and cognitive engagement (17) (p. 10). This pattern aligns with Davenport and Ronanki (2018) and indicates that higher-order AI applications (decision-making, autonomous action) are under-researched.

5. **Under-theorized research**: "Much of the work that has been published to date remains untheoretical. This poses a major issue as the boundary conditions and context in which AI applications are studied are not grounded on established theoretical frameworks" (p. 12).

## Methodology

Systematic Literature Review following Okoli (2015) eight-step guideline:

- **Databases**: AIS eLibrary, Scopus, ISI Web of Science (p. 4)
- **Journals**: 9 IS journals including MIS Quarterly, Information Systems Research, JMIS, EJIS, JSIS, JIT, JAIS, ISJ, IJIM (p. 4)
- **Conferences**: ICIS, ECIS (p. 4)
- **Time span**: 2005-2020 (p. 4)
- **Search string**: Boolean combination of AI terms ("AI" OR "artificial intelligence" OR "machine learning" OR "neural networks" OR cognitive* OR automation* OR robot* OR augment*) (p. 4)
- **Initial retrieval**: 1,877 studies (p. 4)
- **Screening process**: 1,877 -> 1,786 (duplicates/non-English removed) -> 187 (title/abstract/keyword screening) -> 98 primary studies (full-text examination) (p. 5)
- **Two independent reviewers** analyzed studies to combat potential bias (p. 5)

## Findings

**Research methods used** (p. 7):
- Quantitative: 44 studies
- Qualitative: 31 studies
- Literature review: 13 studies
- Mixed method: 8 studies
- Action research: 1 study

**Contribution types** (p. 7):
- Lessons learned: 39 studies
- Framework/method/technique: 26 studies
- Advice/implication: 15 studies
- Models: 6 studies
- Guidelines: 6 studies
- Tools: 5 studies

**Research agenda gaps identified** (Table 14, p. 13):
- AI definition: lack of consensus; need for first-principles approach
- Resurgence of interest: over-focus on technology/performance aspects; societal impacts under-studied
- Machine learning: need to measure effectiveness of ML approaches
- Expert systems: shift to hybrid knowledge bases not well understood
- Robotics: long-term psychological impacts of service robots unexplored
- NLP: effects of advanced chatbot advancement on workers (not just customers) under-studied
- Machine vision: lagging behind other AI advances

## Relevance to Research

1. **Gap identification for agentic AI governance**: The paper's finding that AI governance, risk, and societal implications are under-studied in IS directly supports the positioning of agentic AI governance research as addressing a documented gap in the literature.

2. **Methodological benchmark**: The SLR methodology (1,877 studies, multi-database search, dual-reviewer screening to 98 primary studies) provides a benchmark for the qualitative Phase 1 systematic review in the term paper's research design.

3. **Business value framework**: The three business value types (process automation, cognitive insight, cognitive engagement) from Davenport and Ronanki (2018) could inform how "business performance" is operationalized as a dependent variable. Agentic AI governance's impact may differ across these value types.

4. **Theoretical grounding gap**: The finding that most AI research in IS "remains untheoretical" (p. 12) strengthens the argument for using principal-agent theory and IT governance theory to ground the agentic AI governance study.

5. **Dark-side research gap**: Supports the term paper's argument that while AI capabilities are well-studied, governance of autonomous, goal-directed AI systems (the "dark side" of delegation) lacks empirical evidence.

## Limitations

- Publication bias: focused on select IS journals, excluding non-IS outlets and lower-tier journals (p. 5)
- Search string may not capture all relevant studies due to the breadth of AI terminology
- 15-year timeframe ends in 2020; misses the 2021-2024 surge in generative AI and autonomous agent research
- No quality assessment beyond peer-review status
- Conference papers (65 of 98 primary studies) may include preliminary findings not yet validated
