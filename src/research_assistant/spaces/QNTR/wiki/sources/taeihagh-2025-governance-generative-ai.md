## Summary

Argues that governance of generative AI requires a shift from reactive to proactive and adaptive approaches, with greater public participation and capacity building within the public sector (p. 7). Identifies seven key risk categories and proposes eight governance strategy areas. Explicitly addresses agentic AI control risks, making it the most current and direct treatment of generative AI governance in the literature review.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 7 §Core Argument]

## Key Arguments

### Seven Key Risks of Generative AI (Table 1, pp. 3-5)

1. Hallucination/inaccuracies
2. Jailbreaking/unexpected inputs
3. Data training/validation risks
4. Sensitive information risks
5. Opacity risks
6. Control risks
7. Design/implementation risks

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 7 §Key Findings]

### Control Risks Specific to Agentic AI

"Generative AI systems, particularly with the recent developments in agent-based models, can operate at different levels of autonomy (human-in-the-loop, human-on-the-loop, and fully autonomous)" (p. 4). Extensive deployment and multi-agent interaction "could result in emergent behavior" (p. 4).

"The ability to detect and prevent misuse of the systems is important. If the systems have continuous learning capabilities that could result in undesirable behavior, the ability to swiftly control the system becomes more important, for example in the use of Agentic AI" (p. 4).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 7 §Key Findings]

### Eight Governance Strategy Areas (Table 2, pp. 10-11)

1. Improving data governance/IPR
2. Reducing bias/privacy violations
3. Improving risk management/cybersecurity
4. Reducing negative societal impacts
5. Reducing power imbalances
6. Enhancing public engagement
7. Public sector enhancements
8. Increasing international cooperation

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 7 §Key Findings]

### Governance Mechanisms

Should include: regulatory sandboxes, red teaming, impact assessment, internal auditing as routine practices (p. 8). Voluntary guidelines alone are insufficient; stronger measures including antitrust policies and transparency requirements needed (p. 6).

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 7 §Key Findings]

### Three Autonomy Levels

Human-in-the-loop, human-on-the-loop, and fully autonomous (p. 4). These map onto different levels of workflow control rigidity.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 7 §Key Findings]

### Complex Adaptive Systems Perspective

Janssen (2025), cited in the special issue overview, proposes a complex adaptive system (CAS) perspective for AI governance (p. 11). Offers a complementary theoretical lens.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 7 §Key Findings]

## Methodology

Conceptual/policy analysis paper. Synthesizes literature on generative AI risks and governance challenges, introduces a governance framework, and provides overview of seven articles in the Policy and Society special issue. No primary empirical data collection.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 7 §Methodology]

## Relevance to Research

- **RQ1 (governance practices and risk categories)**: Seven risk categories and eight governance strategy areas provide a taxonomy directly applicable to the qualitative phase.
- **RQ1 (agentic AI specifically)**: Explicitly addresses agentic AI control risks with the quote about "the use of Agentic AI" (p. 4).
- **P1 (governance maturity reduces incidents)**: Proactive governance reduces risks; reactive governance creates regulatory gaps (pp. 7-8).
- **P2 (workflow control rigidity)**: Three autonomy levels map onto different control rigidity levels.
- **Gap the term paper addresses**: Public policy governance vs. enterprise-level governance. The paper focuses on societal governance; the term paper addresses organizational governance.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 7 §Direct Relevance]

## Limitations

- Does not provide empirical evidence from organizations.
- Does not measure governance outcomes quantitatively.
- Does not engage with principal-agent theory.
- Does not address enterprise-level risk outcomes (incident frequency, financial loss).
- Focuses on public policy governance rather than organizational/enterprise governance.
- Does not test propositions about shadow AI or control rigidity.

[Source: data/summaries/literature_review_7papers_analysis.md, Paper 7 §What It Does NOT Do]
