# Research Methodology — DU Research in Management (Barneto 2026)

## Institutional Context

IAE Bordeaux (https://iae-bordeaux.fr) offers both initial training and executive training. Its academic staff includes Full Professors and Associate Professors [Research Methodology.pdf, p.3].

The research laboratory is IRGO (http://www.irgo.fr/), organized into five teams and three research items [Research Methodology.pdf, p.4]:

Teams: AAC, Finance, Marketing, Entrepreneurship, Human Resources.

Research items: Wine & Territory; Corporate Social Responsibility; Digital Management.

IRGO sits within the Department of Research ECOr (Valuation – Behavior – Organization), which is part of the University of Bordeaux research structure. The Graduate Research School is named "Enterprises, Economy and Society" and encompasses Demographics, Economics Sciences, and Management Sciences [Research Methodology.pdf, p.4].

## DU Research Programme Structure

The DBA is structured across three years [Research Methodology.pdf, p.9]:

- Year 1 (DBA 1): DU Research in Management (130 hours). Concludes with a defense of the research proposal.
- Year 2 (DBA 2): Seminars (30 hours).
- Year 3 (DBA 3): Coaching. Concludes with the defense of the thesis.

The Executive DBA delivers a DU (University Diploma) [Research Methodology.pdf, p.9].

The DU Research in Management in Year 1 covers two major teaching units [Research Methodology.pdf, pp.7–8]:

- UE 1 – Principles of Business Research (covering Sustainability Finance & Financial Markets, Financial Information Accounting Reporting and Control, Corporate Governance, Management of Business Models Entrepreneurship and Innovation, Intercultural & Project Management, Human Resources and Organizational Behaviour, International Business Economics, Marketing and Commerce in an International Context — each 12 hours).
- UE 2 – Epistemology and Methodology (60 hours total): Introduction to Management Research (4 hours, Pascal Barneto); Quantitative Methods Applied to Management Science plus software R (28 hours, Jean-Philippe Galan and Jan Schaaper); Qualitative Methods Applied to Management Science plus software NVivo (28 hours, Vincent Maymo and Stéphane Trebucq).

Residential lessons run in Bordeaux from 1 June to 12 June 2026; additional sessions are available in distance learning [Research Methodology.pdf, p.6].

### Year 1 Defense

The defense of research proposals takes place in December 2026, between December 1 and 18. The student presents for 20 minutes, followed by 40 minutes of discussion with the jury. The jury consists of the thesis supervisor and two additional jury members; the defense is conducted by Zoom. The thesis director awards a grade. A score above 10/20 (French grading system) in the DU Research allows the student to continue to DBA 2 [Research Methodology.pdf, p.10].

## DBA Thesis Characteristics

Seven features are identified as essential and characteristic of a DBA thesis focused on fundamental purposes. These are attributed to: Paul Beaulieu, "The Specific Nature of a DBA Thesis," The DBA Thesis Project, p.26 [Research Methodology.pdf, pp.12–14]:

1. The DBA thesis is intended to produce scientific knowledge that meets the needs of management communities and management practice.
2. The DBA thesis must produce new scientific knowledge. It must originate in an assessment and systematic review of available scientific knowledge and a rigorous demonstration of the need for further scientific knowledge. This review focuses on the intersection of practical knowledge and scientific knowledge concerning the problem to be addressed.
3. The DBA thesis must allow for practice and the development of a research attitude that allows considerable space for reflection and critical collective debate.
4. The thesis process and accompanying seminars must allow the development of skills essential for effective scientific communication (written and oral). The candidate may use the literature review to become familiar with editorial rules and practices of scientific media.
5. Since a DBA research project is "naturally" oriented toward and anchored in management fieldwork, the researcher should spend substantial time in practice settings. A "quasi-ethnographic" attitude makes it easier to anchor the work in the field. With a pragmatic methodological orientation, new knowledge and claims should be validated by mobilizing real-world stakeholders.
6. The DBA thesis should enable the candidate to demonstrate mastery of the processes and methods of scientific production and communication. Methodological rigor and a critical attitude are essential to assume the role of a creator of scientific knowledge in complex environments such as organizations and management practice settings.
7. The DBA thesis process is an ideal tool for learning about debates in scientific communities and developing knowledge transfer skills.

## Research Approaches

Three approaches are described [Research Methodology.pdf, pp.22–25], attributed to Kalika M., "The General Framework of Field Studies," Research Methods for the DBA, pp.39–51:

**Deductive approach** (also called the hypothetico-deductive approach): starts by reading the literature — thus, with theories — in order to formulate hypotheses, and then goes into the field to collect data that will make it possible to confirm or disconfirm those hypotheses [Research Methodology.pdf, p.22].

**Inductive approach**: starts the research process in the field and observes facts, then compares them to the literature, conceptualizes, generalizes, and proposes a new theory [Research Methodology.pdf, p.22].

**Iterative (combined) approach**: in practice, management researchers often go back and forth between theories and observations during the research process and combine inductive and deductive approaches. A researcher may start based on field experience, formalize a case or life story, formulate conjectures compared to the literature, then make hypotheses to confirm by returning to the field. Alternatively, hypotheses based on existing literature models may be gathered, data collected, and, in view of disappointing results, new field information collected and reinterpreted to construct a new theory [Research Methodology.pdf, p.23]. Pages 24–25 contain diagrams illustrating iterative feedback between literature and the field [Research Methodology.pdf, pp.24–25; content is image-based on those pages and yields no extractable text].

## Topic Selection Factors

Five factors for choosing a research topic are identified, attributed to manager-researchers [Research Methodology.pdf, p.26]:

1. Their objectives
2. Their resources and competences
3. The opportunities presented by the environment in which they are working
4. The topic's feasibility
5. The thesis supervisor's opinion

## Demonstrating Research Contribution

Four steps are specified for demonstrating the contribution of the thesis [Research Methodology.pdf, p.21]:

1. Summarize the initial research question.
2. Summarize, for each aspect of the contribution, what was already known.
3. Summarize how the results obtained through the thesis add to what was known.
4. Show why it is important from a managerial and/or theoretical perspective.

The slides note: "The literature review is also fundamental because a strong literature review will help you demonstrate the contribution of your thesis. Your contribution is what you add to existing knowledge" [Research Methodology.pdf, p.21].

### Identifying a Research Problem

Attributed to Josserand E., "From the research problem to the research method," Research Methods for the DBA, pp.79–94 [Research Methodology.pdf, p.28]:

- It is necessary to define a general research problem or a context that gives rise to a research problem.
- "There is a problem when you feel the need to bridge the gap between an unsatisfactory starting situation and a desirable ending situation."
- A general research problem can emerge from situations experienced in business units and organizations that offer the possibility for solving a practical managerial problem. It may be an explicitly known or an implicitly constraining symptom.
- The general research problem must be transformed into a precisely problematized research topic. This requires appropriating the notions and concepts related to the general problem (construction of a vocabulary and the necessary understanding of the terms used in the field).

### Commenting on Results

Results must be positioned in the context of country, sector of activity, and company. The researcher must identify the audience (e.g., CEO and executive committee, employees, external members), articulate contributions to knowledge and learning, and specify the managerial impact. The slides state: "This is the most important part of your work" [Research Methodology.pdf, p.31].

## Journal Quality Ranking

A four-level categorization system is described [Research Methodology.pdf, p.17]:

1. Within each field, journals are divided into four ordered categories taking into account criteria of scientific reputation, audience, and impact.
2. Journals are assigned to a category from 1 to 4 in decreasing order of quality.
3. Category 1* gathers generalist journals of category 1 that appear particularly remarkable. Example given: Management Science [Research Methodology.pdf, p.17].

An illustrative example given for Human Resources journals [Research Methodology.pdf, p.17]:
- Journal of Human Resources — Category 1
- Human Resource Management Review — Category 2
- Human Resource Management Journal — Category 3
- Asia Pacific Journal of Human Resources — Category 4

## Research Valorization

Research valorization is defined as "the transformation of fundamental knowledge into new marketable products and services. It takes place through cooperation between public research and companies and through the mobility of researchers. It most often stipulates a private appropriation (exclusive or not) of the products of research" [Research Methodology.pdf, p.15].

The main forms of valorization of public research identified are [Research Methodology.pdf, p.15]:
- The signing of research contracts
- The exploitation of research results (filing of patents, exploitation licenses, etc.)
- The creation of companies by the managers-doctors themselves

A table of audiences and media is provided [Research Methodology.pdf, p.16]:

| Media | Researchers | Managers |
|-------|------------|---------|
| Research Article | Very strong | Weak |
| Managerial Article | Moderate | Strong |
| Oral Communication | Moderate | Weak |
| Book | From moderate to strong | Strong |
| Report | Weak | Strong |
