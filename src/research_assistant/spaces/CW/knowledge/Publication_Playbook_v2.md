# Publication Playbook v2: Structural Patterns from 14 Ivey/Harvard/SMU Teaching Cases

**Synthesized from C2–C15 | Prof. Kakoli Sen's Case Writing Course, IIM Sambalpur**
**Cases analyzed**: C2 (Google SOX), C3 (OTV Lisa), C4 (Vinder Oils), C5 (Café Greenr), C6 (Walmart Sustainability), C7 (Jaipur Rugs), C8 (Clean Air Ltd), C9 (Alpha Advantech), C10 (IKEA India), C11 (Satvic Foods), C12 (Nukkad Tea), C13 (Communication & Accountability), C14 (Vodafone Idea), C15 (Dream Job Turns Sour)

---

## 1. OPENING HOOK CONVENTIONS

Every published case opens within the first two paragraphs using one of four patterns. The protagonist is **always** named with full designation in the first sentence of narrative.

| Hook Type | Frequency | Signature Move | KB Examples |
|-----------|-----------|----------------|-------------|
| **Sensory-moment + tension** | 9/14 (64%) | A sound, a phone call, a sleepless night — then the protagonist's name, title, company, and the problem in one breath | C3: "Beep-beep!…Litisha Mangat Panda…casually tapped open a new WhatsApp message"; C6: "hung up the phone. She was white as a sheet"; C15: "Tyler Lim lay sleepless in bed" |
| **Time-anchored declaration** | 8/14 (57%) | "In [Month] [Year], [Full Name], [Title] of [Company]…" followed by the strategic question | C4: "In January 2022, Aditya Agrawal…was considering a critical issue"; C10: "In February 2024, Susanne Pulverer…"; C14: "In August 2018, Hitesh T.K., the CIO of Vodafone Idea…" |
| **Epigraph before narrative** | 3/14 (21%) | A short quote (founder or literary) set apart, then the protagonist enters | C5: founder quote on global citizenship; C11: Emerson "The first wealth is health"; C12: Roy T. Bennett quote |
| **Industry-context-first** | 2/14 (14%) | Macro trend or sector data in the opening paragraph, protagonist introduced in paragraph 2 | C2: AI/ML adoption in finance before naming Google; C8: air quality industry context |

**Overlap is common** — 6/14 cases combine time-anchor + sensory-moment (e.g., C10 opens with a date AND a store closure decision). The dominant formula is:

> **[Date/Moment] + [Full Name, Title, Company] + [Emotional verb: pondered, wondered, stared, could not decide] + [The dilemma in one sentence]**

### Protagonist Introduction Rules (14/14 cases)
- Full name + title + company name in the **first narrative sentence**
- Personal backstory woven into company history — never a standalone bio section
- Emotional state signaled early: "pondered," "wondered," "stared," "could not decide," "lay sleepless"
- If the protagonist is a family member or second-generation leader, that relationship is established within the first page (C3: Litisha as Jagi's daughter; C4: Aditya in family oil business; C7: NKC's village visits)

## 2. SECTION STRUCTURE & NARRATIVE ARC

### Typical Architecture (6–9 ALL-CAPS sections)

| Section | Purpose | Typical Length | Present In |
|---------|---------|---------------|------------|
| **Opening Vignette** (untitled or case title) | Hook + protagonist + foreshadow dilemma | 1–2 pages | 14/14 |
| **Company Background** | Founding story, growth milestones, key people | 1–2 pages | 14/14 |
| **Industry/Market Context** | Market size, trends, competitive landscape, macro forces | 1–2 pages | 13/14 |
| **Core Problem/Challenge** | The operational or strategic crisis with supporting data | 1–2 pages | 14/14 |
| **Stakeholder Perspectives / Strategic Options** | Differing views, 2–4 explicit alternatives | 1 page | 12/14 |
| **Decision Point / Way Forward** | Protagonist must choose; no answer given | 0.5–1 page | 13/14 |
| **Exhibits** | Data appendix after narrative | 2–5 pages | 14/14 |

### Narrative Arc Pattern
The arc follows a consistent shape across all 14 cases:

```
HOOK (tension) → CONTEXT (company + industry) → ESCALATION (problem deepens) → FORK (options laid out) → OPEN END (protagonist must decide)
```

**Key variation**: C2 (Google SOX) is the only case that wraps up with a quasi-conclusion rather than a fully open ending — it's a governance/compliance case where the framework itself is the "answer." All other 13 cases leave the decision unresolved.

### Page Length & Word Count

| Metric | Range | Median |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Total pages | 6–17 | 10 |
| Estimated word count | ~3,000–7,600 | ~4,500 |
| Narrative pages | 4–10 | 6 |
| Exhibit pages | 2–7 | 3 |

**Outliers**: C7 (Jaipur Rugs) at 17 pages is the longest — justified by its complex social entrepreneurship narrative spanning multiple stakeholder groups. C8 (Clean Air) at 6 pages is the shortest — a focused quantitative/forecasting case.

## 3. EXHIBIT PATTERNS

### Volume Distribution

| Category | Cases | Exhibit Count |
|----------|-------|---------------|
| Light | C10, C11, C15 | 1–2 exhibits |
| Moderate | C2, C4, C6, C8, C12 | 2–5 exhibits |
| Heavy | C7, C9, C13, C14 | 7–13 exhibits |
| **Median** | — | **5 exhibits** |

### Exhibit Type Frequency

| Type | Frequency | What It Looks Like |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Financial data tables | 10/14 (71%) | Revenue, profit, cost breakdowns, P&L summaries |
| Market/industry data | 7/14 (50%) | Market share, export data, sector size, segmentation |
| Comparison tables | 5/14 (36%) | Competitor analysis, feature/price comparisons |
| Process/framework diagrams | 5/14 (36%) | Value chains, lifecycle stages, org charts |
| Organizational charts | 4/14 (29%) | Reporting structures, team composition, skunkworks teams |
| Timeline/milestone tables | 3/14 (21%) | Career progression, company milestones, project phases |
| Product images/photos | 3/14 (21%) | Product photos, store ambience, physical artifacts |
| Email/document reproductions | 1/14 (7%) | C13: actual emails reproduced as exhibits |

### Placement & Integration Rules (universal across all 14)
- Exhibits are **always placed after the full narrative** — never inline
- In-text references use `(see Exhibit N)` — average 4 references per case
- Exhibits are numbered sequentially and titled in ALL CAPS
- Source attribution appears below every exhibit: `Source: Company documents` or `Source: Created by the case authors based on...`
- Financial exhibits almost always appear as Exhibit 1
- The first exhibit referenced in the narrative is typically within the first 3 pages

## 4. VOICE RULES

### Point of View
- **Third-person omniscient**: 13/14 (93%)
- C15 uses a more intimate third-person close to the protagonist's inner thoughts — but still third-person
- First-person appears **only** through direct quotes or epigraphs

### Tense Protocol
- **Past tense**: ~80% of narrative ("was," "had," "decided," "wondered")
- **Present tense**: ~20%, used **only** for industry facts and contextual statements ("The market is…," "India has…," "The industry comprises…")
- **Rule**: Anything the protagonist did or experienced → past tense. Anything that describes the world as it exists → present tense.

### Tone & Register
- Journalistic and neutral — the case **never advocates** for any position
- Descriptive but not literary — no metaphors, no flowery language, no humor
- Standard Ivey disclaimer present in every case: *"The authors do not intend to illustrate either effective or ineffective handling of a managerial situation"*
- Direct quotes from protagonists or stakeholders are used sparingly (1–3 per case) and always attributed
- Numbers are presented factually without editorial commentary ("revenue increased 61 per cent" — not "an impressive 61 per cent")

### Theoretical Framework Integration

| Approach | Frequency | How It Works |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Embedded in situation** | 12/14 (86%) | Case describes a value chain problem without naming "Porter's Value Chain" |
| **Named in passing** | 2/14 (14%) | C2 mentions NIST AI Risk Framework; C6 references GHG Protocol |
| **Textbook-style explanation** | 0/14 (0%) | No case teaches theory directly |

**The rule**: The case provides the raw situation. The teaching note maps it to theory. The case text itself never says "this is a Porter's Five Forces problem."

## 5. TENSION LEVERS

### What Creates Pedagogical Tension

| Tension Type | Cases | Core Conflict |
|-------------|-------|---------------|
| **Survival threat** | C6, C8 | Existential risk — lose a major client or go bankrupt |
| **Growth vs. identity** | C4, C5, C7, C9, C11 | Scale up without losing core values, quality, or craft |
| **Innovation vs. control** | C2, C3, C14 | Adopt new technology while managing risk, trust, legacy systems |
| **Ethics vs. business** | C12, C15 | Social mission or personal integrity vs. operational/career reality |
| **Strategic pivot** | C10 | Abandon current strategy or double down on a failing format |
| **Interpersonal conflict** | C13, C15 | Workplace power dynamics, recognition, accountability |

### How Tension Is Sustained (design patterns across all 14)

1. **No right answer**: Every case presents legitimate arguments for 2–4 paths. The data supports multiple interpretations.
2. **Real data for analysis**: Financial exhibits enable quantitative discussion alongside qualitative judgment.
3. **Stakeholder multiplicity**: Minimum 3 stakeholders with conflicting interests (family, employees, investors, regulators, customers).
4. **Time pressure**: The protagonist must decide "soon" — a deadline, a board meeting, a regulatory notice, a competitor move. Urgency is always present.
5. **Personal stakes**: The decision affects the protagonist personally — their career, family legacy, reputation, or livelihood. It's never purely abstract.
6. **Information asymmetry**: The protagonist (and therefore the student) doesn't have complete information. Some data is missing, some is ambiguous.

## 6. VARIATIONS & OUTLIERS

Not all 14 cases follow the dominant pattern. Notable deviations worth understanding:

| Case | Deviation | Why It Works |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| C2 (Google SOX) | Quasi-closed ending; framework-heavy | Governance/compliance topic requires showing the control framework itself as the "exhibit" |
| C7 (Jaipur Rugs) | 17 pages, 10+ exhibits | Social entrepreneurship spanning artisan communities requires more context to avoid oversimplification |
| C8 (Clean Air) | 6 pages, data-heavy, minimal narrative | Quantitative forecasting case — the exhibits ARE the case; narrative is scaffolding |
| C13 (Communication) | Email reproductions as exhibits | Organizational behavior case where the actual communication artifacts are the evidence |
| C15 (Dream Job) | Intimate POV, A/B structure | Workplace dynamics case uses closer third-person to build empathy; "(A)" signals a sequel |
| C5 (Café Greenr) | Prof. Kakoli Sen as co-author | Demonstrates the course instructor's own publication standard — use as the benchmark |

## 7. PUBLICATION READINESS CHECKLIST

Use this checklist before submitting any teaching case for publication review. Derived from patterns observed across all 14 KB cases.

### Structure
- [ ] Opens with a specific date/moment: protagonist in a concrete setting facing the dilemma
- [ ] Full name + title + company in the first narrative sentence
- [ ] Dilemma foreshadowed in paragraph 1, fully developed in the closing section
- [ ] 6–8 ALL-CAPS section headings
- [ ] 4,000–5,500 words narrative + 3–5 exhibits (target 8–12 total pages)
- [ ] Ends with 2–4 open questions the protagonist must answer (or a reflective pause with unresolved tension)

### Narrative Quality
- [ ] Third-person past tense throughout narrative; present tense only for industry context
- [ ] No theoretical framework names in the case text (save for teaching note)
- [ ] Neutral tone — no advocacy for any option, no editorializing on data
- [ ] Protagonist's emotional state signaled but not melodramatic
- [ ] Direct quotes used sparingly (1–3), always attributed
- [ ] Numbers presented factually without adjectives ("increased 61%" not "an impressive 61%")

### Exhibits
- [ ] All exhibits placed after narrative, never inline
- [ ] Numbered sequentially, titled in ALL CAPS
- [ ] Source attribution below every exhibit
- [ ] Financial summary as Exhibit 1 (if applicable)
- [ ] In-text `(see Exhibit N)` references woven naturally into narrative (minimum 3)
- [ ] At least one financial table, one market/industry data exhibit, one comparison or process exhibit

### Pedagogical Design
- [ ] 2–4 explicit strategic alternatives presented (no "right answer")
- [ ] Minimum 3 stakeholders with conflicting interests
- [ ] Time pressure / urgency established
- [ ] Personal stakes for the protagonist (career, family, livelihood)
- [ ] Sufficient data for both quantitative and qualitative classroom discussion
- [ ] Decision point is genuinely ambiguous — reasonable students will disagree

### Publication Mechanics
- [ ] Ivey-standard copyright disclaimer and authorship statement
- [ ] "The authors do not intend to illustrate either effective or ineffective handling" disclaimer
- [ ] All currency amounts defined with exchange rate footnote on first use
- [ ] Endnotes for all factual claims, data sources, and quotes
- [ ] No fabricated data — all exhibits sourced from company documents or public sources

## 8. APPLICATION TO SAI TEX LTD. (ICHALKARANJI CASE)

Based on the playbook patterns and the KB materials (AKP interview responses, Case Idea Template, draft narrative), here is the structural scaffold:

### Recommended Opening Hook
Use the **sensory-moment + time-anchor** combination (the dominant 64% pattern):

> *October 2025. The night shift at Sai Tex Ltd. was silent. Avinash Kisanrao Patil stood alone on the factory floor, the ten Rifa Rapier machines idle around him. His phone showed two notifications: a ₹1,20,000 interest debit from the bank, and a WhatsApp message from his eldest worker, Shivmani Sharma, asking if there would be work next week...*

This mirrors C3's WhatsApp-notification hook and C15's sleepless-night tension while grounding in AKP's actual financial data.

### Section Map (targeting 8–10 pages + 4 exhibits)

| # | Section Title | Content Source (KB) | Target Length |
|---|--------------|-------------------|---------------|
| 1 | *(Untitled opening vignette)* | AKP financials: ₹3L revenue, ₹4L costs, ₹1L monthly loss | 1.5 pages |
| 2 | THE MANCHESTER OF MAHARASHTRA | Ichalkaranji cluster: 50,000+ looms, 12,000 air-jet/rapier, migrant labor shift (AKP interview) | 1.5 pages |
| 3 | SAI TEX: A DECADE OF WEAVING | Founded 2010, 10 Rifa machines at ₹12L each, job-work model at 18 paisa/pick, Pride Co-operative membership | 1.5 pages |
| 4 | THE PERFECT STORM | US tariff → 40% export drop → local competition surge → job rates below breakeven; energy costs rising 10–15% every 2 years | 1.5 pages |
| 5 | THREE PATHS FORWARD | Option A: Green Technical Pivot (₹3.6Cr air-jet + solar ₹50L + Sale-Purchase model ₹25L); Option B: Hybrid Lean (retrofit ₹40–50L); Option C: Exit (machines at ₹2L resale each) | 1.5 pages |
| 6 | THE DECISION | Bank requires personal bungalow as collateral; family supports but opposes more stress/debt; 18 workers' livelihoods at stake | 0.5 pages |

### Exhibit Plan

| Exhibit | Type | Data Source |
|---------|------|-------------|
| 1 | Monthly P&L breakdown | AKP's actual numbers: ₹3L revenue, ₹1.2L interest, ₹80K electricity, ₹2L payroll |
| 2 | Green Pivot financial projection | Air-jet at ₹35/mtr vs. current ₹10/mtr; 4x production speed; debt service at 14% vs. 10% green subvention |
| 3 | Ichalkaranji cluster data | 50,000 ordinary looms, 12,000 air-jet/rapier, 3,000 auto looms; worker migration patterns |
| 4 | Strategic options comparison | Side-by-side: investment required, risk level, debt impact, timeline, worker impact for all 3 options |

### Tension Levers (mapped to KB patterns)

| Lever | Sai Tex Instantiation | Parallel in KB Cases |
|-------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Survival threat | ₹1L monthly loss, 14% interest on ₹1.2Cr debt | C6 (Walmart supplier), C8 (Clean Air) |
| Growth vs. identity | Job-work tradition vs. Sale-Purchase pivot | C4 (Vinder quality vs. market), C7 (Jaipur Rugs craft vs. scale) |
| Family/SEW stakes | Bungalow as collateral, family opposes more debt | C9 (Alpha Advantech founder vision), C4 (family oil business) |
| Worker welfare | 18 families dependent, Shivmani's 10-year loyalty | C7 (artisan communities), C12 (Nukkad employee inclusion) |
| Regulatory pressure | Energy cost escalation, cluster-wide competitive squeeze | C6 (sustainability mandates), C10 (IKEA format constraints) |
| Information gap | No balance sheet exists; AKP tracks via personal ledger | Creates authentic ambiguity for classroom analysis |
