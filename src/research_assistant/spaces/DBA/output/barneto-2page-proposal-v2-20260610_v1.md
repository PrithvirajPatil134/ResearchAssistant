# Organizational Structural Transformation in Technology Firms During the Generative AI Transition: A Longitudinal Archival Study

**Prithviraj Patil** | DBA Programme, IAE Bordeaux / IIM Sambalpur

## Research Aim and Questions

The aim of this research is to document and explain the patterns of organizational structural change in major technology firms during the generative AI (GenAI) transition period (2022-2027), using punctuated equilibrium theory as an interpretive lens.

The primary research question asks: What patterns of organizational structural change characterize major technology firms during the GenAI transition period?

Three sub-questions guide the empirical work:

1. What specific structural elements (management layers, role categories, divisional boundaries, reporting relationships) changed between 2022 and 2027 in the sampled firms?
2. Do the sampled firms exhibit convergence toward common organizational forms, or divergence into distinct structural archetypes?
3. To what extent do the observed structural changes align with the predictions of punctuated equilibrium theory (stable period, disruption, transformation, new equilibrium)?

## Background and Gap

Between November 2022 and mid-2026, large technology companies restructured at a pace without recent precedent in the sector. Layoffs.fyi data records over 260,000 tech employees laid off in 2023 alone, with restructuring charges running into billions across several firms. These reductions co-occurred with the emergence of GenAI capabilities: companies cited AI-related strategic shifts in their restructuring communications. New roles appeared (Chief AI Officer, Head of AI Platform), management layers were consolidated, entire divisions merged, and reporting structures were redesigned.

MIT Sloan Management Review (2024) observed that "previous waves of technology have ushered in innovations that strengthened traditional organizational structure. Not so for generative AI and large language models." McKinsey's 2025 Global AI Survey found organizations are "redesigning workflows as they deploy gen AI and putting senior leaders in critical roles such as overseeing AI governance."

The phenomenon is visible. What the academic literature lacks is a systematic, longitudinal, cross-company empirical record of what these structural transformations actually look like: what changed, in what sequence, and whether firms are converging toward similar forms or diverging into distinct archetypes. Existing contributions are largely theoretical, practitioner-focused survey pieces, or conceptual frameworks not yet tested against archival evidence from real firms. Existing peer-reviewed work has not, to date, drawn on longitudinal corporate disclosure data to track the actual structural changes in specific firms across the GenAI transition, and this study addresses that gap.

## Theoretical Lens

Punctuated equilibrium theory (Tushman and Romanelli, 1985; Romanelli and Tushman, 1994) proposes that organizations alternate between long convergent periods of incremental change and brief reorientation episodes of discontinuous, frame-breaking transformation. The theory predicts a specific sequence: a deep structure persists during convergence; an environmental jolt triggers reorientation; reorientation involves simultaneous changes across strategy, structure, power distributions, and control systems; a new convergent period then begins around the transformed structure.

Applied here: technology firms operated within a broadly stable organizational model from approximately 2010 to 2022 (scaled hiring, layered management, functional divisions, growth-driven headcount). The emergence of GPT-4-class models and their rapid enterprise adoption constitutes a candidate environmental jolt. The restructuring activity of 2023-2026 may represent the reorientation period. Whether a new convergence has begun is an empirical question this study aims to address.

## Method

The study employs a longitudinal, multiple-case archival design using qualitative content analysis of publicly available corporate documents.

**Sample**: Six to eight publicly listed US technology firms with market capitalization above $50 billion, selected for publicly documented restructuring activity during 2023-2026 and variation in primary business model (cloud/infrastructure, enterprise software, social/advertising, hardware/devices).

**Study period**: Fiscal years 2021-2027, with 2021-2022 serving as the pre-transition baseline.

**Data sources**: SEC 10-K and 10-Q filings (employee counts, organizational descriptions, restructuring charges); earnings call transcripts (CEO/CFO commentary on structural changes); WARN Act filings (division-level layoff notices); public CEO communications; corporate press releases and blog posts.

**Analytical steps**: (1) Structural mapping per firm per year documenting headcount, divisions, named leadership roles, and management layers. (2) Change coding using a framework derived from Mintzberg (1979) and Galbraith (2014), categorizing each change as addition, removal, or modification of structural elements. (3) Within-case narrative construction, mapping each firm's trajectory against punctuated equilibrium's predicted sequence. (4) Cross-case comparison identifying common changes, divergent paths, and potential archetypes.

One design question remains open: whether and how to classify firms along an adoption-intensity dimension (aggressive vs. incremental adopters). Four possible operationalizations exist (AI capital expenditure ratios, CEO strategic framing, product portfolio pivot indicators, external analyst classification). Each carries a circularity risk: classifying firms by a criterion that correlates with the structural outcomes under study. I intend to resolve this through a focused literature review of adoption-intensity classification methods, punctuated-equilibrium operationalizations in archival settings, and precedents for using SEC data in organizational structure research. This classification question is the immediate next intellectual step; whether it enters the final design depends on what the literature supports.

## Expected Contribution

For theory: the study tests punctuated equilibrium against a contemporary, high-visibility organizational transition, providing archival evidence of whether the GenAI disruption follows the predicted sequence or departs from it. For practice: it provides executives facing similar pressures with an evidence-based map of what early-moving firms actually did, not what consultancies speculate they did. For method: it demonstrates the use of public corporate disclosures (10-K filings, earnings calls, WARN Act data) as a valid source for organizational structure research, offering a replicable template for future archival studies of organizational design.
